=== azukitcg-roadmap | AzukiTCG Roadmap | What Azuki TCG’s Q4 2026–Q2 2027 roadmap reveals about its ambitions
Azuki TCG Roadmap Analysis
Azuki TCG's August 2, 2026 roadmap offers a structured path from the current launch window through the middle of 2027. It lays out concrete product drops and competitive events.
AZK-02 Set Release (Q4 2026)
AZK-02 arrives roughly six months after the launch of Gates Awakened. The expansion will test the Azuki team's ability to progress the metagame while limiting power creep. A successful AZK-02 should keep strong cards from the AZK-01 relevant while introducing new Leaders, Gates, and strategic options.
1st Anniversary Collection (Q4 2026)
The 1st Anniversary Collection is scheduled for the same quarter. Anniversary products usually prioritize collectors over pure competitive players. As seen with the Special Collection Volume 1, which released online on July 29th, players with participation points should receive pre-orders, as Azuki continues to prioritize them. 
Placing it alongside AZK-02 creates a dual-product window that can appeal to both audiences simultaneously.
Garden Arena: Midwest Regional (Q4 2026)
On the competitive side, the Garden Arena Midwest Regional also arrives in Q4. The Midwest tournament expands beyond the initial Los Angeles event, lowering the travel barrier for players in the central United States and testing whether the organized-play system can handle multi-region logistics. High attendance along with a well-produced event will prove the reasons to grow the competitive scene of Azuki. 
International Expansion (Q1 2027)
With the international expansion next on the roadmap, we move to Q1 2027. This is the most impactful event on the roadmap. Successfully launching in new markets would enlarge the player pool, secondary market, and cultural reach of the game. At the same time, international growth requires reliable local partners, distribution, translation, and tournament support. The timing, after two sets and several domestic regionals, is sensible, giving the team time to refine systems at home before exporting them. The execution of the international release will heavily influence whether Azuki TCG remains a primarily North American title or becomes a broader one.
Garden Arena: Regional (Location TBD) — Q1 2027
Another Garden Arena Regional, at a location still to be determined, is also scheduled for Q1. Hopefully, an East Coast location is chosen to designate three zones across the lower 48. This event will continue the expansion of the competitive ladder. Leading up to the National Championship and any other Invitationals, it will serve as an important qualifier.
Azuki Invitational II 2027 (Q1 2027)
The same quarter includes Azuki Invitational II 2027. Invitational events gather the strongest performers from the regionals and points leaderboards leading up to the event. A well-run second Invitational would reinforce the sense that Azuki has a high-level competitive scene rather than simply a collection of larger local tournaments.
AZK-03 Set Release (Q2 2027)
In Q2 202,7 the AZK-03 set will release. Azuki will be nearly a year and a half old by this point. Proper samples of real-world data on balance, player behavior, and metagame trends will be influencing the cards released in this set. The third expansion will need to address any remaining issues from the first two sets while still giving players new tools to create decks with. Its arrival just before the biggest tournament of the year means the National Championship will be shaped by how well AZK-03 lands.
Garden Arena: Regional (Location TBD) — Q2 2027
That same quarter features another Garden Arena Regional at a location still to be announced. This event is positioned late in the season and functions largely as a final major qualifier before the championship. It runs close to both a new set release and the national event, creating a compressed, high-stakes period in which players must adapt quickly to new cards while competing for qualification.
Garden Arena: National Championship (Q2 2027)
The roadmap ends with the Garden Arena National Championship in Q2 2027. The national title event combines all of the previous regional and invitational events into a season. The prestige, prize pool, production values, and fairness of the qualification process for this championship will largely determine how the wider TCG community judges Azuki's competitive scene. A successful national event would show Azuki TCG moving from promising new game to established title.



=== how-and-why-to-become-an-azukitcg-judge | How and Why to Become an AzukiTCG Judge | None
The competitive scene for AzukiTCG’s set 1 begins in Pasadena, California, on August 15th with the Garden Arena regionals at CoreTCG. With this massive event kicking off the competitive calendar, Judges are needed desperately for all regional and high-end events planned for the year. This article will run through both “How do I become a Judge?” and “Why should I become a Judge?” to answer all of the questions you could have about being an official AzukiTCG Judge.
How do I become a Judge?
To become an AzukiTCG Judge, you must pass an online test that is 33 questions long, getting no more than 3 wrong. You must get an A on your exam to become a Judge. If you take your test and fail, you must wait a month before taking the test again. Tests are administered by a proctor in 30-minute windows (so no cheating).

The first step any interested person must take is joining the Azuki Judge Discord server. Once there, you are free to ask current judges or prospective judges any questions you have about both the tournament rules and the game rules. Before taking your test, it is recommended to read both at least 3 times. 

Once any prospective Judge is all studied up, they must wait until new Judge testing times drop for signup. Currently, no new judge testing slots will be dropped until after the Pasadena Garden Regional. 

Why should I become a Judge

Along with being paid to help run events for your favorite trading card game, Judges receive exclusive judge promo cards for [Ed] and [Rei] that are pictured below. The Ed and Rei cards are full-art versions of their normal cards that come with the “Gate Keeper” stamp. Judges will receive 2 of each card on top of their pay.

If the exclusive promo cards aren’t enough, you get the experience of participating in the competitive scene regardless of your skill level. Book knowledge is all that matters to be a judge, and considering you passed the test, you have it.

For more information, visit https://tcg.azuki.com/judge.

An aggressive deck is simply more linear, making it a great choice for me to play in my first tournament. For aggro I have good threats. For control, I need specific answers. I was interested in playing earth in this tournament as I love playing heavy control decks, however my general lack of knowledge of Azuki TCG caused me to opt to play an aggressive deck. Inevitably fires combination of Zero (a leader with a 0 cost ability) quality 1 drops, and access to extreme levels of cheap burn was extremely enticing to me. I have a lot of reps on playing burn in many other TCG’s and the deckbuilding principals from those games would carry over greatly to Azuki TCG.What deck building principals?My deck had 1 major goal: Deal 20 damage as fast as possible.To facilitate this I needed to ensure my deck would never stumble on deploying its threats. The extremely high density of 1 drops in combination with Azuki TCG’s powerful mulligan essentially guarantees I will hit my curve.This means I needed my deck to hit an opener like this every game:Going first: 1 drop on turn 1, 2x 1 drops on turn 2Going second: 2x 1 drops on turn 1, 2x 1 drops on turn 2Plus I will ideally have another 1 cost unit I can put into play with Rushfire Gate on turn 2.Turn 3 this deck will usually play Cinderwake Ritualist or Pekiro.Turn 4 and on this deck is looking to play burn spells every turn until it kills the opponent, runs out of cards, or dies. Rushfire Gate: The decks centerpiece and most powerful tool. If you can activate Rushhfire gate 2 or 3 times in a game you are cooking. Fanatic Kindler also has the great ability where it can be sacced on play to deal 1 to an entity of your choice. === explore-the-lore-ii-raizan-and-shao | Explore the Lore II: Raizan and Shao | Everything we know about Raizan and Shao came from 'Enter the Garden' and 'Sisters.' That is, until the Azuki TCG.
Recently, I did a deep dive into the Azuki TCG cards to determine whether or not the game really expanded on established lore. The first article of the series, which focused on the mysterious Black Jade Mahjong Club, audited whether or not the cards and game mechanics provided new details or insights into the Azuki lore.
And hey, Feb.Tea himself said to keep digging, so I was either onto something and they should hire me…or they’re making it up as they go and they should hire me. 
(My rates are pretty competitive but we can negotiate!)
In Explore the Lore Part II, I’m narrowing the scope to focus on two of Azuki’s most recognized characters: Raizan and Shao.
Not only are they the first Leader cards introduced to the TCG, but they’re also among the few characters with established lore within the Azuki universe. 
My approach
For this installment, I needed to take a different approach than the one I used for the Black Jade, which was a topic of which no concrete information existed prior to the TCG. I had to start from zero (no, not the character) and take small clues from the cards to form larger theories.
In contrast, we know a decent amount about Raizan and Shao already because of the stories Azuki has released. So for this one, I’m taking what we know about the characters, then using the cards to supplement the info and look for cohesion and thematic consistency.
If there is really a Lore bible as claimed, then these elements should appear in the TCG. 
(Don’t worry, there’ll be fan theories as well)
But, first…
Shao & Raizan: What We Actually Know
Most of the confirmed information comes from the following sources: 
“Sisters” - Animated short
“Broken Reflections” - Enter the Garden, Episode 2
Sisters
Shao and Raizan are sisters who grew up together in the Alley (the Alley itself will be the subject of a future post). They lived a hard life, struggling to get by and fighting to survive. Raizan took on the role of protector, doing whatever it took to keep them afloat. 
At some point, Raizan discovered the Garden, a mystical world accessible by portaling in through gates. Raizan started crossing into the Garden and bringing back relics she found to the Alley, which she would sell for money. At first, the Garden wasn’t framed as someplace dangerous. 
Then the sword changed everything.
The Sword
Among the relics Raizan brought back was a katana. She warned Shao that the katana was dangerous, which was confirmed when Shao started hearing the sword speak to her. Shortly after the discovery, mysterious entities, referred to as "Shadows," appeared in the Alley through the same gate. Raizan fought the Shadows back through the gate, but she vanished, and the gate along with her.
Shao’s Perseverance
At first, Shao waited, hoping her sister would return as she always had. Days stretched into weeks, and weeks stretched into years. No sign of her sister. Shao began to question whether any of it was even real. Wondering if she had simply dreamt up the Garden. 
Then one day, the sword reappeared. The charm attached to the hilt revealed a single red bean, which she took to pass through the gate and enter the Garden. Portaling through the gate itself took an undetermined amount of time, with Shao unclear about how much time she spent traveling between worlds.
Was it a momentary, metaphysical flash? Or was it an eternal illusion? After going through what felt like an infinite cycle of time…I reached another world.”
Once in the Garden, she pushed forward, the memories of her sister motivating her to keep going. At some point in the timeline, she finds a Wanted poster of Raizan, who looked drastically different than when Shao had last seen her. From the anime, it seems that Shao came across multiple different gates on her journey which would take her to vastly different geographies and locations. 
The Reunion
Shao eventually reunited with Raizan, though she didn’t realize it at first. She encountered a masked woman blocking her way to another gate. The two battled, with Shao unmasking the mysterious figure to reveal Raizan in her new form. 
They continued to fight and they also ran — a lot — and then the episode ended on a cliffhanger.
Raizan and Shao in the TCG
Now for the fun part.
We’ll start by familiarizing ourselves with the cards that directly reference Raizan and Shao, then take a closer look at the card mechanics and details.
Raizan cards
Not including alt art cards, there are four cards that reference Raizan directly by name. One Weapon, one Leader, one Entity, and one Spell.
[Ikazuchi] [Raizan] [Raizan 2] [Raizan's Riposte]
There’s also a fifth Raizan card that doesn’t reference her by name, or at least not the one we’re familiar with: Rei.
[Rei]
We’ll come back to the Rei-Raizan connection later in the card analysis section.
Shao cards
Not including alt art cards, there are three that reference Shao directly by name. One Leader, one Spell, and one Entity.
[Shao] [Shao's Perseverance] [Young Shao]
Tying the cards to established lore
Raizan Deck
The Raizan Lightning deck is fast tempo and aggro. If you’re playing Raizan, you’re swinging those weapons as hard as you can and as frequently as you can. This is reflected within the animated short, with Raizan charging off to fight Alley thugs to keep herself and her sister safe. The band-aid on her face adds more color to her aggressive personality, attacking opponents regardless of risk.
Raizan was also the one who went into the Garden with reckless abandon to bring back things to pawn, leaving Shao behind. 
Even looking at the Raizan Entity card, her attack power (4) is higher than her health (3). Her Leader ability allows players to charge other entities to attack immediately with powerful hits.
Shao Deck
In sharp contrast, the Shao Water deck is slower-paced and more about synergy with the other cards in the deck. 
The [Young] Shao Entity card reflects that contrast with its attack power (2) and health (2) being the same, and lower than that of Raizan. Shao’s card gets a boost in attack and health with more board control, which takes time and setup.
In the anime, Shao went through years of waiting and searching to find her sister, likely honing her abilities over time. Her tenacity is literally reflected in the name of her Spell card, Shao’s Perseverance.
I also think it’s a nice detail that the Shao Starter Deck includes both Young Shao and the younger version of Raizan, aka Rei — I’ll talk more about this in the next section, I swear. 
In the anime episode, Shao has several flashbacks to childhood memories of her sister; memories that she carries with her throughout the whole story.
So the fact that the adult Shao, represented in the Shao Starter Deck, includes the childhood versions of herself and her sister is pretty poetic. I’m going to assume this was intentional and give credit where credit is due.
Lore theories
If you’re still reading, thank you / I’m so sorry. 
We’ve checked off the established lore and cohesion, so it’s time to put on our Sub-Reddit fan theory hats.
The Rei-Raizan Connection
Now, I don’t have an encyclopedic knowledge of the Azuki IP like some of my fellow holders do, but to my understanding, this is the first reference to Raizan by a different name. 
But going back through the animated short and anime, Shao’s narration almost always refers to Raizan as “sister,” except for the very end of the short when Shao discovers the Wanted poster and asks: 
“Raizan, what have you become?” 
Something corrupted Rei and turned her into Raizan during the years she spent in the Garden. We don’t know what triggered the change, but I think the most likely bet is Raizan’s sword: Ikazuchi.
Ikazuchi corrupts Rei
When looking at the cards, Ikazuchi and Raizan (Leader & Entity) have clear synergy. In the hands of any other Leader or Entity card, Ikazuchi is a big sword that deals 4 damage. In the hands of Raizan, however, it does 5 damage plus 1 damage to each entities in your opponents Garden.
Applying these card details to the anime, we know that Ikazuchi wasn’t the sword that Rei brought back from the Garden. We first see Rei holding the sword when she’s already Raizan many years later. The sword very clearly empowers her, but I think it’s also what corrupted her.
This then leads me to wonder how common it is for weapons in the Azuki universe to empower their wielders. Looking at all the Weapon cards in the TCG, we see that every weapon has a special effect. 
Of course, the TCG is a game and these effects are almost certainly purely for the sake of gameplay.  Mainly mentioning this to segue into my next theory.
Shao’s katana empowers her
I think at some point, in the upcoming manga or anime, we’ll learn more about a special ability that Shao possesses. And I think it’ll come from her own sword, as was the case with Raizan.

=== the-art-of-redirection | The Art of Redirection | Which Cards can ping damage and how can it be useful?
Since the release of AZK-01, players have scoured every card looking for ways to make decks faster and faster. Reasoning correctly that the best defense is offense. This effort culminated in “Zero RushFire”, a deck known for redirecting damage using the ability [Zero (Leader)] to ping damage to the opponent’s garden or leader through either  [Pekiro] or [Cinderwake Ritualist] . 
In this episode of Deck Tech, we'll explore all the different cards that can ping damage (any card with an on-play damage effect or an ability that deals damage without using its attack) to further the explosiveness of Zero RushFire (or any deck), answer all the questions you might have about strengths, weaknesses, and drawbacks, and rank every card out of ten.(We will be excluding anything I deem as return damage for this overview and any sacrifice cards; cards like [Black Jade Vault Master] and [Dropline Station] will have their own article dedicated to them)

Caravan Guard - AZK01-005
The [Caravan Guard] is the first card on our list that can “ping” damage. Caravan Guard has an “On Play” effect; this effect pings one damage to an entity in your opponent’s Garden. In published decks, the Caravan Guard is used most commonly in a pair (2/4 available slots for the card taken up). Being a 2/2 card, the Caravan Guard is moderately hard to remove from the Garden, its only drawback being the 4-IKZ cost it takes to play.
7/10
Lounge Siren, Saeko - AZK01-57
Lounge Sire, Saeko has an on-card ability that pings damage simultaneously to both Gardens at the start of every turn (as long as it is in the Garden itself). 

Saeko has a couple of very advantageous traits. It is a part of the Scorchweaver subtype; this means that [Glass Blower, Hokuto] , a very common card in fire decks, can search for it. Along with this, it is a 1-IKZ cost card that has 1 gate power, meaning it works well with [Rushfire Gate], the meta gate for fire decks. Along with this, Saeko is best played in combination with either [Pekiro] or [Cinderwake Ritualist], the cards that make Zero RushFire what it is, to ping both damage to the opponent’s Garden/leader. Despite these advantages, Saeko is an extremely underused card, being featured in 6 RushFire decks.
This is because of its weaknesses. Saeko is a 1/1 card, meaning it is easily taken out of the garden by any opposing pings and attacks. Along with this, it takes itself out if no other cards are present in the Garden with it at the start of the turn (while also pinging one damage to the opponent’s Garden). Finally, Seako is hard to set up correctly. In almost all cases, it is better to have a card like [Spice] taking up max slots in your deck.
6.5/10
Firebrand Renji - AZK01-61
Bringing his flaming spray-paint cans to the list, [Firebrand Renji] is a 3 IKZ cost that has an on-play card effect that pings damage to an opponent’s Garden or Leader. 

Starting with the strengths, Renji is a 1/3 card; being incredibly difficult to remove from the Garden while only being a 3 cost is a huge plus. Renji is a Pyreskin subtype, meaning [Ruby] can search for him. There is no arguing against Renji being an absolute tank in the Garden. Being a 3 health card, Renji can be pinged by [Zero] and gain the damage buff to become a 2/2. So far, Renji is featured in seven decks, where in the majority he makes up the majority of 3 IKZ-cost cards.

Renji made this list because his on-card ability pings 3 damage if he is attacked by three different damage sources in one turn. Renji’s main weakness is his on-card ability. It is nearly impossible to accomplish that feat, and it is entirely in the opponent’s hands to make that decision. With Renji only dealing 1 damage a turn, it is simply a better decision to ignore him and leave him on the garden to prevent the 3 damage ping. 
4/10
Enzo - AZK01-62
Being one of two Uncommon 6-IKZ cost cards (the other being [Sanzu's Envoy]), [Enzo] holds a unique position inside the AzukiTCG. Featuring an on-play ability that pings both 3 damage to any Leader or entity in any Garden, Enzo is dangerous from play.
Enzo’s strength lies in his damage (both attacking and ping) and health. Enzo is also a Scorchweaver subtype; he can be searched for by [Glass Blower, Hokuto]. His on-card ability is amazing, providing a free 3 damage if played in the Garden.
There are two drawbacks that I can see: Enzo is a 6-IKZ cost card, and to use his on-card ability, he must be played in the Garden, meaning you can’t RushFire gate him up to receive a free 2-IKZ cost card such as [Black Jade Warlord].
6.5/10
Zero - AZK01-64
[Zero] is an 8-IKZ cost entity. When Zero enters the Garden, he deals 2 damage to all entities in every Garden. Zero is the entity version of [Firestorm]. Zero is the strongest card Fire has to offer, at 7/7. Zero’s ability is amazing, and so are his stats; the only drawback is being a 7-IKZ cost. Getting to the 7th turn of a game as a Fire deck is hard, and isn’t entirely worth the deck slot dedicated to him.
4/10
Gin and Tonika - AZK01-79
The 2nd neutral card on this list is [Gin and Tonika],  a 6-IKZ cost card that gives the opponent the choice of either taking 3 damage to their Leader or letting you draw 2 cards. Being a 3/4 card, Gin and Tonika is hard to remove from the Garden and can deal serious damage. Holding a gate-power of 3 as well boosts this card to being played in countless decks posted. Holding an amazing ability and stats, Gin and Tonika are only held back by being a 6-IKZ cost (which is still miles better than [Zero] at an 8-IKZ cost).
9/10
Omen Peddler - AZK01-114
The first rare card to make the list, [Omen Peddler] is unique from the cards displayed above. Omen Peddler’s on-play ability pings 2 damage to an entity in your oponent’s Garden if it is played in the Alley. Being the first card that must be played in the Alley to take advantage of this ability, Omen Peddler has a very narrow style of play. The 3-IKZ cost card bosts a 2/1 stat line which is respectable, damage can be done with this card. Omen Peddler is also a Scorchweaver subtype allowing it to be searched for by [Glass Blower, Hokuto].
There is one glaring drawback to this card, to take advantage of its on-play ping ability, you must play it in the garden. This 3-IKZ cost does NOT have any gate power. It is one of 5 cards that has the same cost and gate power, unlike any of the other cards though, Omen Pedler must be played in the Alley to make use of its ability. None of the other cards have this problem and it takes Omen Peddler down several notches.
2.5/10
Crazed Arsonist - AZK01-115
The [Crazed Arsonist] card holds both a sacrificial on-card ability (which will be covered in a future sacrifice article) and a attacking on-card ability. The 2-IKZ cost card will always ping 1 damage to both players’ Leaders when it attacks. Depending on the stage of the game this can be either really good or really bad.
In the early game when both players’ Leaders are at or near full health, it is worthwhile to ping both down by 1 damage. In the late game where every turn could mean you lose and where every health point matters, it is not the best idea to ping yourself for 1 damage just to ping your opponent for 1 as well. Essentially, this card is useless in the late game because of its mandatory ping every time it attacks. 
([Glass Blower, Hokuto] can search for Crazed Arsonist, due to its Scorchweaver subtype, which is a slight plus)
3.5/10
Tenmoku Daiki - AZK01-116
This 4-IKZ cost, 3/2 tank, pings 3 damage to your leader on-play. Everything else about this card is amazing. Being a 4-IKZ cost, being 3/2 (where most cards would be a 3/1), having charge as soon as it enters the garden. There is no arguing [Tenmoku Daiki] is a strong card. It just comes in with a self inflicting [Fire Orb] that doesn’t inflict damage on your opponent. The stats on this card are amazing but the madnetory self ping is not.
5/10
Bandit Ringleader - AZK01-118
Right off the jump, this card is amazing. Everything about it screams perfect, and balanced. c[Bandit Ringleader] is a 4-IKZ cost 2/2. Ringleader also has 2 Gate Power, statistically this card is perfect. (It’s ability is NOT bad by any stretch, its middle of the road).
Bandit Ringleader has an on-play ability that deals 1 damage to an entity in your garden (if you don’t have another entity in the garden you ping the Ringleader which will bring it down to a 2/1 until the end of your turn) and IF you have already played 2 cards this turn (like with [Cinderwake Pursuer]), you deal 2 damage to an entity in your opponent’s Garden. 
On paper this ability is good, however it is just so hard to actually pull of. You must be in at least the 6th turn of the game to take advantage of this. That is not good, especially with the fact that Fire deck games are usually over turn 5. Despite this Ringleader is in 6 published decks so it is seeing some use.
7/10
Silver Current, Haruhi - STT01-6
The first card on our list from the starting decks, [Silver Current, Haruhi] (or granny) is a 2-IKZ cost card that bosts being a 1/2. When attacking Haruchi deals 1 additional ping damage that almost always goes towards your opponent’s Leader. This card is phenomenal and is used in the meta Lightning deck. This is the first truly fundamental card on our list. Okay stats but great for the IKZ cost, amazing and simple ability, Naruhi is a GREAT card.
10/10
Scorchveil Shinobi, Suzuka
The last card on our list is [Scorchveil Shinobi, Suzuka]. When you play this card with [Zero (Leader)] as your leader it will ping 1 damage to all entities in any Garden and give the surviving entities in your Garden +1 attack until the end of the turn on-play. Suzuka is a 6-IKZ cost card with a 3/3 stat line, along with the “Charge” ability.
Unlike previous 6-IKZ cost cards, this one has the most upside. Especially with the stipulation for ther ability, when you use the best Fire deck leader you can do this, considerably better than using [Enzo] and just fireballing yourself.
8/10
The Art of Redirection
There are no remaining cards that I deem to be held under this category of “ping”ing. Future articles will be coming for sacrifice damage as well as taking returning damage. Happy deck building!

Element Locking System
Azuki TCG decks are mono-element — your main deck cards must match your Leader and Gate's element. The deckbuilder enforces this automatically:
When you add a Leader or Gate, your deck locks to that element

The card pool automatically filters to show only matching element cards plus Neutral cards

Element filter buttons for other elements become locked (visually indicated)

A colored element banner appears above your filters showing the locked element

For example, adding [Raizan] as your Leader locks your deck to Lightning. You'll only see Lightning cards, Neutral cards like [Gou the Iron Judge], and IKZ cards in the gallery.
IKZ Visibility When Locked

=== beginner-s-guide-collecting-azuki-tcg-what-to-collect-2 | Beginner's Guide: Collecting Azuki TCG (What to Collect) | Part 1 in Cali's Series of Beginner-Friendly Articles
Hi, I’m Cali! If you’re an expert at collecting Azuki TCG cards, you can stop reading. This article is for beginners. PART 1 BACKGROUND: What is the Azuki Trading Card Game?
The Azuki Trading Card Game (Azuki TCG) is an original anime-inspired card game developed by Azuki Labs, based on the popular Azuki multimedia and digital collectible universe. Set One, Gates Awakened (AZK-01), officially launched in stores nationwide on June 26, 2026. The first primary release features four Starter Decks (Raizan, Shao, Zero, and Bobu) for four different elements and the 148-card booster set. Players build 50-card decks, along with their selected Leader, Gate, and 10 IKZ cards, to battle against an opponent and reduce their Leader's HP from 20 to zero. (Yes, I crafted this description.)

PART II WHAT TO COLLECT: What’s Out There to Collect?
Even though Set 1 of the Azuki TCG was recently released in June 2026, there are already a lot of things to collect out there. For a beginner, it might get overwhelming, so let’s start at the beginning, in chronological order. I’ll explain what was released in case it piques your interest and sparks an idea for what you might want to collect. 
The rule of thumb for collecting is that there are no rules. Collect what you like. If you’re in it to make money, then you might need to pay attention to rarity, scarcity, pricing. If you’re in it to collect what speaks to you, then you do you, whatever that looks like. Either way, you’ll need to know what’s out there. This is where this article comes in. === making-beanz-work-maybe | Making Beanz Work (Maybe)? | A deep dive on playing Benzai with neutrals.
Beanz in Benzai
""Benzai’s Best Beanz (BBB)”

Quick shoutout to TK, very thankful for chatting with him (a fellow perma Flutter enjoyer), who called out the math and how it favors you when you really need to mulligan for one card.What if now you had 4 more that could also dig for it ([Link] searching [Healing Flutter])?
Cards and why it works:
[Alley Guy]→ is the reason the frog leader is utilized. On 3 IKZ you can Alley Guy to set up a 2-cost draw-frog or [Mirage Frog] if needed or a second Alley Guy  (1\2s are great!)

[Link] → can search itself meaning 4 targets is actually 8 targets. Now we can fold in 1 cost [Penny] as blockers and solve the blocker problem if not using [Foamback Crab].

[Jay] → is 2\3 for 3 ikz when online. That’s insane. He lives through Quicksand, he cannot be wrong stepped, he trades (and lives) [Black Jade Crewleader], [Cinderwake Seer], etc. etc.

[Hayabusa Itto] → has immense value in being a 1 cost unit with 1 gate power. Bouncing back flutter early is tough to do when many units have 0 gate power. Itto helps us also maintain normal water consistency tools.

[Mo] → is actually a very good neutral, and checks his own limits by having an element-based effect, so he gets away with having a 4\4 stat line. Mo in my opinion is amazing in the recall gate as you get hydro gate for one turn, and can also use the flutter you get back with him that turn. You get to play the 6-cost guy and re-flutter which is HUGE in this deck. Or grab a strike and make them play around it.

[Black Jade Brawler] → is pretty much this decks [Tenshin], it closes out those annoying quick games with surprise damage, returns you a Flutter in the process (or [Commune with Water]), and can look to board clear going into Mo – [Mizuryuu Fist Master] – [Sōryū no Rin (Alt Art)] turns. Even more neutral synergy.

Remember:
We do not run Beanz to play Jay, we play Jay because we are running Beanz for Flutter, and we can use Jay if we want. We do not solely rely on conditional synergy.

Brainstorming the Curve
Turn 1 → You have options to Link to dig deeper for Flutter or Itto. I love to Itto in Alley against Fire or Lightning and just end, so that on Turn 2 you can heal the 2 the Raizan or Zero deck just did to you with Flutter, then return it to hand with Itto when you gate him in next turn.
Itto in Alley can also live rent-free inside an opp's head that knows this angle and won't self-ping their Dagger or use their Rushfire Gate to throw a card away for free, assuming you have Flutter access.


Turn 2 → You can Alley Guy if Jay on 3, or Mirage Frog to clear board, or Benzai if nothing else and no Jay and just need a good 1\2 unit. Making conscious decisions here, based on what your next 2 turns will be, is crucial. You have the ability to just end with 2 IKZ up here too if justified in the scenario. Commune checks the coin Crewleader rush play, and pretty much everything else because of Sundering Strike. Maybe your only 2-cost units are Water and you need Jay synergy on 3.

Turn 3 → The game opens up hard as you follow up on what you committed to on Turn 2. Frog leader lets you play two 2c units through an Alley Guy discard if you wanted to scale. Conversely, another 2-drop + Flutter, or even 1-drop + Flutter + 1 IKZ up, are great ways to extend the clock against aggressive playstyles.

Very important to Flutter and return Flutter as many times as you can because you can only gate once per turn.
Lets analyze more than we should:
"This is a rabbit hole I don't want to leave."
Beanz are love. Beanz are life. Everybody loves Beanz. If you have been craving a way to play these little demons in a competitive setting, then you're in the right place!
This isn't just a dive into Beanz, but I wanted to dive into neutral cards as well, where they excel, and their limitations.
I am not a pro TCG player. I've competed at some pretty sick tournaments, but I am by no means some sort of top-ranked player on any leaderboard. I do it for the love of the game.
I do, however, have a pretty solid understanding of TCG mechanics and am a deckbuilder who loves to just share what I learned. My list likely isn't perfect, my reasoning might have good arguments against it, but I am very proud of this deck and wanted to share what I personally learned.
DISCLAIMER: If you want die-hard meta Fire or an optimized Raizan deck, then Beanz might not be for you. But if you want to bring a new deck to your next session and take the Bean pill, then you're in the right place!
The Problem we are solving:
"Okay but why?"
There's usually one thing flying through my head whenever I'm about to take an L to some nonsense Fire Orb or a 40-billion attack damage swing from a Crewleader, which is:
        "How do I heal more?"
        "How do I heal more while still playing good cards?"
        "How do I heal more while still making my deck consistent?"
        "When does Domino's close?"
        "What in the world does this have to do with Beanz?"
Healing Flutter is extremely cheap healing in terms of IKZ cost. You get 2 health per 1 IKZ spent when fluttering, and you will pay vastly more for the same amount of healing via any other means.
 I know what you’re thinking big dog:
“That’s not fair to say – Flutter does not impact the board at all, does not come with a body, and you only get the 2 in one card (there’s a real defined ceiling in how much heal you can get from flutter in one full game.)”
                -You, the very smart reader, probably.
But Frog gang is here to solve that problem and introduce a very real way to solve concerns no other leader can dream of having access to.
Truthfully, it's the Gate that gives you the answer, but the Frog is how you maintain value similar to Cashback Gate (Hydromancy), and Beanz are how you actually hit your win condition.

The Real TL;DR:
The key is understanding you already low-key want to play Neutrals in the Gromp deck.
The Neutral Dilemma/Benefits
"Why do these cards look like they suck?"
Neutrals are, well, exactly that: neutral element cards that can be used in any element. Because of that, they need to be made from the perspective of, "If any one single leader has access to this card, does it break the game?"
From a balance perspective, neutrals need to be made with the idea that any Gate/Leader can use them. Conversely, they are a smart way to give leaders tools to handle oppressive situations from other leaders without buffing any one specific element (CC Sundering Strike).
It's very fitting for Beanz to be neutral, a very real mascot-like identity for no single element.
WE DON'T GATEKEEP BEANZ
This introduces a fun puzzle for Beanz. You can play them anywhere, so what is the best way to play them? Is there a good way to play them?
        "Is crate rate good for Brewmaster Charge in Raizan?"
        "Is Gus Defender in Earth the move?"
        "When does Domino's close?"
        "Is Hydromancy value + Top Beanz the future?"

I’m a fan of the recall gate, and I hope you enjoyed it!

=== explore-the-lore-the-black-jade | Explore the Lore: The Black Jade | None
Admittedly, when I first read Zagabond’s tweet about Azuki lore being expanded through the TCG, I didn’t buy it, but I’m chalking this up to your standard down-NFT-market reflex. But I wanted to see for myself, so I started to look through the beautifully-designed cards for signs of real lore expansion.
I decided to start this — we’ll call it a lore audit — with the mysterious Black Jade Mahjong Club (BJMC). The Black Jade is ingrained deeply into the culture of Azuki, but not much is known about what they are, who they are, or what they do. 
Besides a handful of traits in Elementals, a jacket that Zagabond rocked, and the occasional Easter egg, we haven’t had a proper starting point from which to dig deeper. That is, until Azuki TCG was released.
I took a look at the 11 Black Jade cards in the Azuki TCG to see what insights I could glean.
First, a quick refresher on these cards:
Before getting into theories, it’s worth clarifying how I’m reading these cards. I’m not treating stats as lore, but I am treating repeated mechanics, restrictions, and role design as intentional signals and signs of lore expansion. When the same motifs and ideas show up across multiple cards, it’s something worth looking at more closely. 
Initial Observations
Besides three neutrals, every Black Jade card is in either Fire or Lightning domains. Both Fire and Lightning are known for their aggro and fast tempo play styles. Thus far, there are no Black Jade cards in Water or Earth; this immediately sets Black Jade apart from factions built around balance, patience, or long-term sustain.
Additionally, everyone seems to have a specific role within a hierarchy, and most of those roles involve moving resources or burning themselves out for the next play. None of these cards are monks, or guardians, or elders; titles which would imply a cultural history tied to a religion, lineage, or specific geographic location. All of the named Black Jade roles are ones you’d find in an organization. 
The Hierarchy
The different members of the Black Jade seem to be organized around roles and specific functions. The roles imply a kind of structure not bound by Elemental domains, but instead, a common purpose. You’ve got couriers, pawnbrokers, recruits, vault masters, warlords, and decoys. 
Lower-rank units
Couriers and Recruits, both of whom are neutrals, seem to be low-level, expendable members. Maybe ones who haven’t yet earned their stripes or proven their loyalty. Crewleaders are mid-level units with more power and higher cost, though this depends entirely on whether or not they have a weapon to wield. Interestingly, Black Jade Couriers, which imply delivering items from one place to another, do not have any Gate power that would let them portal weapons into the Garden. 
Higher-rank units
The title of Vault Master implies status or leadership, which lines up with the Vault Master’s higher IKZ cost, higher stats, and special ability. And when you factor in the central aspect that weapons play in Black Jade cards, specifically in Lightning decks, we might surmise heavier responsibility that comes with the role. Despite this, even Vault Masters seem to be expendable, as their ability set makes them defenders that tank damage in the place of other units. The mechanism also damages an enemy leader when attacked, which adds more context to the expendability concept. 
In regard to Leader cards in Black Jade, Zero stands alone. Zero’s ability lets him damage himself to buff up his troops. Even the highest authorities do not seem to be exempt from self-sacrifice. 
Clues From Card Mechanics
A significant portion of Black Jade weapon and card effects revolve around discarding, sacrificing, or inflicting self-damage to meet a goal. Recruits discard weapons to search for other ones or set up weapons for later use. Crate Rats are designed to mill cards to increase the chance of getting more weapons into circulation. Pawnbrokers mill cards to potentially reclaim weapons. Warlords give up their body and life to destroy another entity. Decoys also seem to exist to be destroyed. Even the Black Jade Dagger is a weapon that allows a player to swing for higher damage after the player damages themselves. 
When looking closely at Fire, Zero is a powerful entity and Leader that will dish out punishment and power up his units, even if that power-up comes at the cost of the entity’s life or health. 
All of these point to an organization that sees its members as expendable, or an organization whose members are willing to do anything to further the cause. This theory is further bolstered by the lack of healing or sustain within the card mechanics for these cards.
Lore Audit Results
Fine, Zagabond wasn’t making things up. Based on the available Black Jade cards and game mechanics in the TCG, they do seem to incorporate clues and pieces of lore that come together thematically. A broader look at the available cards (to be explored further in this series) does point to motifs and recurring themes that tie together. The artwork, the details, and mechanics of Azuki TCG weren’t simply slapped together. The Alpha Deck release, along with the upcoming Set 3 and Set 4, provide much needed color to the Azuki universe. 
TL:DR
Black Jade is structured around roles and functions

Currently only associated with Fire and Lightning

Weapons are central to the Black Jade

Sacrifice and self-damage are core to gameplay, and perhaps the Black Jade’s guiding principles

Even high-ranking roles are designed to be expendable

The Black Jade hit strong and fast, and they disrupt

The missing presence of Water and Earth may point to a rejection of balance and long-term sustain

What’s Missing?
Naturally, these are all theories until Azuki releases more lore through the TCG, upcoming manga, and more published content. We still have no explicit origin story for the conflict between domains and their factions.
Zoomed in, we’re also missing any clear ideological statement. We can see how the Black Jade operates, but not why. What is their explicit mission in the Garden? We also don’t yet know if Black Jade rejects those domains outright, or if this is simply a slice of the organization we’re seeing so far. The absence of Water and Earth remains unexplained — is this on purpose, or have the cards simply not yet been released? 
I’m excited to see what additional details we can discover with the other cards in circulation, as well as newly released cards that will continue to expand the IP.

=== your-first-azuki-tcg-tournament-everything-you-need-to-know | Your First Azuki TCG Tournament: Everything You Need to Know | A friendly guide to preparing for and enjoying your first competitive event
So you've been playing Azuki TCG with friends, building decks, and now you're ready to take the next step—your first tournament. Congratulations! Tournaments are an incredible way to meet fellow players, test your skills, and immerse yourself in the competitive side of the game. If you're feeling a mix of excitement and nerves, that's completely normal. This guide will walk you through everything you need to know so you can show up confident and have a great time.
Before the Tournament: Getting Your Deck Ready
The most important thing to prepare is your deck—but not just the cards themselves. Here's your pre-tournament checklist:
Your 50-card main deck, 1 Leader, 1 Gate, and up to 10 IKZ cards

Opaque card sleeves for your main deck (no see-through backs!)

Different sleeves for your Leader, Gate, and IKZ cards—they must be easily distinguishable from your main deck

Check your sleeves for damage or marks that could identify specific cards

Only official Azuki TCG cards are allowed—no proxies or custom cards

Double-sleeving is allowed as long as the card fronts remain unobstructed. Take a few minutes the night before to inspect your sleeves and replace any that look worn or marked.
What to Bring
Beyond your deck, here's what you'll want to have with you:
Counters or tokens to track damage and effects

A playmat (optional, but makes gameplay smoother)

Pen and paper for life tracking

Water and snacks (keep them off the play table during matches)

A positive attitude!

Note: Electronic devices, food, and drinks are not allowed on the table during matches. Your deck box can stay on the table, but make sure it doesn't contain any extra cards.
Understanding Match Structure
Tournament matches typically have a 30-minute timer. This might feel like pressure at first, but it's usually plenty of time for a complete game. Here are the key things to know:
Play at a reasonable pace—aim to take meaningful action every 30 seconds or so

If you arrive more than 5 minutes late to your assigned table, you forfeit the match

You can decide who goes first by any fair method (usually a die roll)

Communication and Etiquette
Good communication makes for good games. When you play a card or activate an ability, announce it clearly. Keep your cards on the field organized and visible to your opponent. If you're ever unsure about something, just ask!
A few important rules about information:
Your hand and deck contents are private—you don't have to reveal them

You must answer truthfully if asked about public information (cards in play, discard pile, number of cards in hand)

Intentionally lying about game state is a serious violation

Remember: your opponent is a real person who loves the same game you do. Win or lose, be gracious. A handshake and a "good game" go a long way.
Shuffling Your Deck
Before each game, you'll shuffle your deck to randomize the cards. Your opponent is allowed to observe you shuffling and can "cut" your deck afterward (or even reshuffle it themselves). This is standard practice and not a sign of distrust—it's just part of fair play.
When Time Is Called
If the round timer ends mid-game, don't panic! Here's what happens:
You get two more turns (or five minutes, whichever comes first)

If no winner is determined, the player with more Leader health wins

If still tied: most cards in deck → most entities in Garden → most in Alley → most cards in hand

If somehow still tied, the player who took damage last loses

Judges Are Your Friends
Judges are there to help, not to punish. If you have any questions about rules, card interactions, or game state, raise your hand and call for a judge. They can clarify rules and resolve disputes. Just remember: judges can't give you strategic advice—they're there for rules questions only.
Don't be embarrassed to call a judge, even for "simple" questions. It's better to get a clear answer than to play incorrectly.
After Each Match
Once a match ends, both players are responsible for reporting the correct result. This is usually done at a designated station or through an app. Double-check that the result is recorded correctly before walking away.
Between rounds, you'll have time to relax, grab a snack, and chat with other players. This is one of the best parts of tournaments—the community! Don't be shy about talking to people, asking about their decks, or sharing your own experiences.
Final Tips for Success
Get a good night's sleep before the event

Eat breakfast—you'll need energy for a long day of thinking

Know your deck inside and out—practice makes perfect

Check for any banned cards or errata before the event

Focus on learning and having fun, not just winning

Your first tournament is a learning experience. You might make mistakes, and that's okay! Every top player started exactly where you are now. The goal is to have fun, meet people, and get better with each game you play.
Welcome to competitive Azuki TCG.

=== every-point-of-damage-matters | Every Point of Damage Matters | Zero Midrange Deck Tech
The Slow Burn | Zero Midrange Deck Tech
When I started building with [Zero (Leader)], I wasn't looking to make another Fire aggro deck.
Fire already has some of the best removal in Azuki TCG, so instead of trying to race every opponent, I wanted to lean into that strength. The result is a midrange-control deck that slows the game down, controls the Garden, and eventually creates one or two explosive turns that completely swing the board in your favor.
Every card has a purpose. Some buy time. Some remove threats. Others quietly build toward the turn where [Scorchveil Shinobi, Suzuka (Alt Art)] turns your defensive board into an offensive one.
After a lot of games and plenty of tweaking, this is the version I've had the most success with. It's still evolving, but I wanted to share what's been working for me and hopefully give other Fire players something new to try.You're not trying to race.
You're trying to make every point of damage matter.
Game Plan
This deck is all about controlling the Garden.
Your early turns are spent trading resources efficiently while making combat as awkward as possible for your opponent. [Rei], [Fanatic Kindler], [Sundering Strike], [Collateral Burst], [Rainy Day Assassin], and [Omen Peddler] all help keep opposing boards under control while your utility entities buy valuable time.
One thing you'll notice is that many of the entities have 2 Health.
That's completely intentional.
They survive Zero's ping, trade well in combat, and give you more flexibility when sequencing your damage effects.
The longer the game goes, the stronger your position usually becomes.
Buying Time
Some of the least flashy cards end up doing the most work.
[Mocking Dummy] is one of the biggest reasons this deck can comfortably play the long game. As a 0/2, it reduces incoming damage, forces awkward attacks, and frequently buys the extra turn you need. Later in the game, Suzuka and Zero can even turn it into a legitimate attacker.
[Healing Flutter] is another card that tends to surprise people. Healing 2 life doesn't sound impressive, but in practice it often buys exactly one more turn. For this deck, that's usually enough.
[Rei] also overperforms. Combined with Zero's leader ability or your various ping effects, Rei efficiently cleans up opposing 1-health and 2-health entities while helping maintain control of the Garden.
Every Point of Damage Matters
The deck is built around getting as much value as possible from every damage effect.
[Zero (Leader)] isn't simply giving something +1 Attack.
He's damaging one of your own entities to set up [Ragefire Gate] while also buffing it.
[Collateral Burst] isn't just removal.
Sometimes it's killing an opposing threat.
Sometimes it's damaging one of your own entities to set up future plays.
Sometimes it's both.
Almost every card has multiple jobs, and sequencing those effects correctly is where the deck really shines.
Firestorm Changes Everything
Firestorm is much more than a board wipe.
Only Silk Tongue Velya, Pekiro, Enzo, and Suzuka naturally survive [Firestorm], and that's by design.
Most of your other entities have already generated the value you wanted from them.
Velya continues protecting your leader, Pekiro can redirect the damage that would be dealt to it, while Enzo and Suzuka remain as immediate threats after the board has been cleared.
Pekiro's redirect also allows Firestorm to finish off larger threats that normally survive two damage.
Meanwhile, Cinderwake Ritualist converts the damage it takes into another two damage anywhere before it leaves the field.
Those interactions allow Firestorm to remove cards like [Mizuki], [Gin and Tonika], and even [Stone Masked Ancient] with the right setup, turning what looks like an even trade into a massive advantage.
Suzuka Wins Games
If Firestorm stabilizes the game, Suzuka usually ends it.
One of the strongest lines is playing Suzuka into the Alley instead of directly into the Garden.
Her On Play ability still deals 1 damage to every entity in both Gardens, often clearing your opponent's smaller entities before she ever enters combat.
If Pekiro or Cinderwake Ritualist are already on the field, that single point of damage can become even more through redirects or additional damage, letting you finish off larger threats that survived the initial hit.
Then every other entity in your Garden gets +1 Attack until end of turn.
Even Mocking Dummy suddenly becomes a real attacker.
After clearing the board, portal Suzuka into the Garden with Ragefire Gate.
Suzuka enters with Charge, Ragefire Gate gives another damaged entity +Attack equal to Suzuka's Gate Power, and what looked like a stalled board suddenly becomes an overwhelming attack.
Card Choices
Silk Tongue Velya
One of the biggest reasons this deck can play a true control game.
As of today, [Silk Tongue Velya] is Fire's only Defender, and she puts in serious work.
A 1/4 body is already awkward to attack through, and being able to redirect attacks gives you an option that most Fire decks simply don't have. Instead of racing, you can protect your life total, force unfavorable attacks, and buy the extra turn your deck is built around.
She also survives both Firestorm and Suzuka, making her one of the few entities that naturally sticks around through your biggest swing turns.
Her Once Per Turn ability is easy to overlook, but it adds up quickly. Whenever she takes or deals damage, both leaders take 1 damage. Between blocking, Zero's ping, Collateral Burst, Suzuka, and normal combat, Velya provides reliable chip damage that slowly puts your opponent within range of your finishers.
She's rarely the flashiest card on the board, but she's one of the hardest-working cards in the deck.
Rainy Day Assassin
Probably the most unconventional card in the list.
This deck relies heavily on damage to control the board, so cards that reduce, ignore, or redirect damage can completely disrupt your game plan.
[Rainy Day Assassin] ignores all of that.
Instead of trying to damage those entities, it simply destroys them.
Some of the biggest targets include:
Fire
• [Pekiro]
• [Cinderwake Ritualist]
Water
• [Foamback Crab]
• [Serene Fist, Misaki]
Earth
• [Kale]
Beyond those matchups, it's simply an efficient answer to any troublesome 3-cost or less entity.
Omen Peddler
Another card that tends to surprise people.
On paper, it's only a 2/1.
In practice, it's usually a two-for-one.
Played into the Alley, [Omen Peddler] immediately deals 2 damage to an opposing entity, often removing an early threat or setting up another removal spell.
If left alone, it can attack for another 2 damage before eventually being cleared by one of your own board wipes.
Sequencing Tip
Unless you need the immediate 2 damage from attacking, try to leave Omen Peddler in the Alley. Its On Play ability still gives you immediate value, and keeping it out of the Garden means it survives your own Firestorm and Suzuka turns. After you've cleared the board, simply portal it into the Garden on a later turn to add another body and continue applying pressure.
Toughest Cards to Face
[Quicksand] is probably the single card I least want to see. Since the deck relies on a number of 2-health utility entities, a well-timed Quicksand can undo a lot of your early-game setup. The good news is that many Earth lists have moved away from running multiple copies. If Quicksand ever becomes a staple again, this deck gets noticeably worse. Even then, you're often able to rebuild quickly with threats like Enzo, Suzuka, and Silk Tongue Velya, which all survive Quicksand and can help stabilize the board again.
Changes to Consider
-2 [Omen Peddler]
+2 [Fire Orb]
This is the biggest change I'm currently considering.
Fire Orb originally stayed on the cutting room floor because dealing 3 damage to yourself felt completely opposite to what this deck wants to do. Most games you're trying to preserve your life total, control the board, and survive long enough to eventually swing everything back in your favor.
That said, Fire Orb may simply be the stronger card.
It gives the deck additional reach to finish games, cleanly answers larger threats, and provides another powerful finisher once you've stabilized.
The self-damage is definitely real, but the ceiling on the card may be worth the tradeoff.
Final Thoughts
This isn't your typical Fire deck.
You're not trying to flood the board and attack every turn.
You're trying to control the Garden, force favorable trades, and slowly build toward a turn your opponent can't recover from.
Every point of damage matters.
Every turn presents multiple lines.
And sequencing is everything.
Most Fire decks ask one question:
"Can I kill you before you stabilize?"
This deck asks a different one:
"Can you survive after I stabilize?"
In my experience, the answer is usually no.

=== aez-s-shao-deck-guide-q1-2026 | Aez's Shao Deck Guide, Q1 2026 | A comprehensive breakdown of the only Shao deck to place in the top 16 of the 2026 Azuki TCG Invitational Tournament
Brief Tournament Recap:
Going into the tournament fully knowing that Zero decks were going to be everywhere was daunting. JCM (game dev) also kept hinting that he saw lots of success with Bobu decks in the play sessions leading up to the tourney. On top of all that, all of the players that I had bounced ideas off of for Shao deck builds and playstyle had completely switched to playing Zero or Raizan. Deck Build
Leader: Shao’s Leader ability allows you to use 1 IKZ to respond to an opposing leader or entity’s attack by 1 until the end of the turn.
Gate: The Hydromancy Gate allows you to portal in an entity from the Alley into the Garden and untap the number of IKZ up to the Gate Power of the portaled card.
Given the current meta of early game aggressive playstyle accelerated by the existence of Zero’s deck’s unblockable Scarlett infiltrate damage (7 damage on turn 5!) and Fire Orb damage (5 damage on turn 4!), a typical control Shao deck simply isn’t fast enough to deal enough damage before turn 5 in order to stand a chance against Zero.

Because of how few cards in the Water card pool have high attack relative to its IKZ cost, you tend to want to flood the board as much as possible so that on turn 3-4, you can start swinging 4+ damage while controlling the board effectively enough to reduce the opponent’s board progression. To accomplish this, you want to maximize the use of your Hydromancy Gate in order to get back as much IKZ per turn in order to effectively play your cards to control the board.

1. Core Cards
The core of my water deck centers around 3 cards: Foamback Crab, Mirage Frog, and Serene Fist Misaki. Foamback Crab
Foamback Crab is a 2-cost defender with 1 Gate Power and 0/1 stats. It can’t be damaged by card effects which makes it especially nice to have vs the ping-heavy decks that you often see with Zero and Raizan. The general rule of thumb here is to have it out in the Garden by the time you’re expecting a blockable 4+ damage threat. Silver Current Haruhi (this is the single best use for Mirage Frog vs Lightning decks, as it neutralizes or delays their turn 3, earning you back a valuable turn as Shao).

Alley Thug (just delaying the 2/1 entity from being able to attack).


Fire:
Black Jade Warlord (bouncing this delays the 4-damage potential including its sacrifice ability when it can attack).

Enraged Howler (this is a common target for players to use the Zero ability to get the +1 bonus attack damage; getting it off the board prevents that potential 3-5 damage swing including portaled entity damage).

Cinderwake Seer (always good to delay the 2 attack damage entity’s ability to swing).

Fanatic Kindler (common target for Zero ability + portal bonus daamge).

Alley Thug.


Earth: Earth players tend to portal in entities to constantly keep defenders up to protect leader health until they can scale and play their big 7+ IKZ cost entities. As such, it’s good to use the Mirage Frog bounce on any 1-2 IKZ cost entity they have set up as a defender in order to deal chip damage to the Bobu leader health. If Wobbly Cabbage Cart is a defender, prioritize bouncing it so that you don’t accidentally destroy it; its card effect can destroy a valuable Foamback Crab or Serene Fist Misaki.


Serene Fist, Misaki
Serene Fist Misaki is a 2-cost entity with 0 Gate Power and 2/1 Stats. It can’t be damaged by card effects which makes it one of the best mid-game attackers for the Shao deck, especially at such a low cost. Generally, you want to protect it as best as you can - so you either keep 1 IKZ available to prevent a 1-damage attack opposing entity from taking it out, or making the opponent use 2 entities, or a costlier entity, to take it out, thereby helping with opposing board clear. 
Because Serene Fist Misaki doesn’t have any Gate Power, you ideally want to play it using your Fumiko ability (explained later), or play and portal in a different entity during the turn to maximize your IKZ usage that turn.

2. Card Combos
There are a handful of cards that I included in my deck with the purpose of playing them in a combo with other cards to maximize their effectiveness in building up board control. These cards are Fumiko, Young Shao, and Red Bean.
Fumiko + 1-2 cost entity
Fumiko is a 3-cost entity with 1 Gate Power and 1/2 stats. On play, you may bounce any entity from your Garden back to your hand and then play any entity that costs 2 IKZ or less from your hand, without any addition IKZ cost. I almost always look for an opportunity to bounce a 1 IKZ cost entity to play one of my core 2 cost entities (like Mirage Frog, Foamback Crab, or Serene Fist Misaki). This allows me to effectively:
play 5 IKZ worth of cards (3 for Fumiko and 2 for the other entity) on turn 2.

spend only 2 net IKZ (3 for Fumiko, then you regain 1 IKZ by portaling the Fumiko into the Garden from the Alley) for this exchange.

return a Hayabusa Itto or Mizuto back to my hand so I can use its On Play searcher ability again later on.

If you know you’re going to use the Fumiko to trade the 1 cost entity to play a 2-cost entity, try to protect your 1-cost entity by leaving 1 IKZ up the turn prior, or by not attacking with the 1-cost entity so it remains untapped and thus protected from direct opposing attacks.
*Note: If your opponent destroys your 1 cost entity before you can use it for Fumiko, it is NOT the end of the world; just adapt and find a different way to use your Fumiko. For instance, I have used my Fumiko many times by attacking with my Serene Fist Misaki, then playing Fumiko to bounce my Misaki and replay it in the Garden, untapped. This effectively protects my Misaki the next turn. Be flexible!

Young Shao + Red Bean
Young Shao is a 4-cost entity with 3 Gate Power and 2/2 stats. When you have 2 or more additional entities in your Garden than your opponent, the Young Shao’s stats become 3/3. Red Bean is a 1-cost spell that gives charge to an entity that costs 4 or less IKZ.
On turn 4 or 5, I like to play Young Shao in the Alley, portal in for 2 IKZ back, and then spend 1 of them on a Red Bean to attack leader health to get in additional and rushed chip damage to start keeping up with the pace of damage of Lightning and Fire decks, or sniping an opposing entity and surviving, thus removing a tricky opposing entity and adding another entity into your own Garden. The key here is SURVIVING this snipe.
As a 3/3 Young Shao, think about taking out an opposing 2/2 and 2/1 entities.
As a 2/2 Young Shao, think about taking out an opposing 1/2 entities. 
*Note: If you don’t have a Red Bean, don’t be afraid to just play the Young Shao as is, and maybe play a 1/1 entity as well on turn 4. It’s still valuable to continue flooding the board with entity and slowly gaining the upper hand on board control.

3. Defense
Even though the current meta calls for hyper aggression early and mid game, there are certain defensive cards that exist in the game that, while often costly, can create the momentum swing needed in the game to regain board control. For Shao, the cards I use for this purpose are Water Orb, Mizuryuu’s Torrent, and Pulled Under.
Because of the high cost of these cards, they’re to be used very situationally and carefully - a misuse of one of these cards can often backfire on the user, leading to a loss.

Water Orb
Water Orb is a 1-cost response spell that reduces a leader’s or entity’s attack by 2 after you discard a card from your hand. Because this Shao deck has two cards whose effects rely on discarding a card (Mirage Frog and Water Orb), when you have one of these two cards in your hand, it’s ideal to constantly identify the least priority cards in your hands to discard. This identification comes with repetition in playing the deck and figuring out your ideal play style and flow with the deck. 
There are a few instances where I look for opportunities to use a Water Orb:
to protect a Mizuki, Young Shao, Trade Guild Cavalry, or Misaki on my board from being taken out by an opposing entity.

to reduce late game damage to my leader’s health to survive just one more crucial turn.

Truthfully, Water Orb usage depends on the player and their play style - there really isn’t one single “correct” way to use it.

Mizuryuu’s Torrent
Mizuryuu’s Torrent is a 4-cost spell that allows you to choose 2 entities that add up to 5 or less IKZ cost in your opponent’s Garden and have them bottom deck them both in any order they choose. Given the current early game aggro meta, it can be difficult to find the space to play this spell as by turn 4, you’re trying to build up board control and presence as a Shao deck user. However, here are some instances where using Mizuryuu’s Torrent can definitely recoup the loss of using 4 IKZ that turn from most ideal to less ideal:
Water:
Trade Guild Cavalry (this card essentially allows for 5 damage to be dealt each turn with its card effect).

Mizuki (most Shao players are only able to play 2-3 per game - bottom decking 1 is a huge blow to a Shao’s late game board control and damage output).

2x Serene Fist Misaki (this duo will do 4 damage per turn and is very difficult to take out as Shao without losing board presence).


Lightning:
Black Jade Vault Master (taking out a 5-cost 1/3 stats defender is huge, especially since most of your entities can’t one shot it. Its ability dealing 1 damage to your leader every time it is attacked adds up, and can’t be blocked).

2x Silver Hand Haruhi (taking out 2 entities that can deal near-unblockable card effect damage is huge value, and allows you to start filling your Garden with your many 2-health entities without fear of them being taken out every turn by Silver Hand Haruhi’s card effect damage).

Silver Hand Haruhi + Black Jade Crew Leader (situational; only do this if the opponent is close to ending game and you want to buy yourself an extra turn).

Besides these, Mizuryuu’s Torrent will just slow down the needed board progression to win against a Lightning deck user.


Fire:
It’s usually ideal to just keep swinging at Zero’s leader health because of how easy it is to spam high damage dealing 1-2 cost entities and spells as a Fire deck user.

Sometimes, using Mizuryuu’s Torrent on a Cinderwake Ritualist + Enraged Howler is nice, but again - only as a last resort option. More board progression to outscale Fire decks’ damage output over time is important.


Earth:
You effectively want to use Mizuryuu’s Torrent mid-late game to get rid of as many defenders as possible so that you can swing your damage at the Bobu leader’s health, unencumbered.

Because of the Earth portal’s ability, just about any entity in the Bobu deck can turn into an entity. Therefore, you want to prioritize using Mizuryuu’s Torrent to take out the defending entities with the most protective card effects when attacked. This is what I tend to prioritize from most to least:
Chillax defender + 1-cost defender (Chillax giving every other entity in the opposing Garden when attacked is incredibly difficult to get through)

Kale defender + 1- or 2-cost defender (Carapace’s reduced damage intake is tough to take out with Shao deck’s generally low attack-state entities)

Shroom Tender defender/Yojin defender/Koyama Farm Plowman defender (all 3 have high enough attack stats to destroy most Shao deck entities that it defends against)


Try to Mizuryuu’s Torrent as many defenders (limit 2) it takes to get to 5 IKZ cost to get maximum value out of your 4-cost spell.


*Note: Mizuryuu’s Torrent is probably the single most game-changing card in any given game it is played, for better or for worse. Make sure not to overuse it in situations where playing other cards would be more beneficial. I personally rarely play it before turn 5 because of how much using 4 IKZ for a non-entity sets back my board progression as a Shao deck user.

Pulled Under
Pulled Under is a 6-cost spell that allows you to choose a 7-cost or less card in your opponent’s Garden to bottom deck. Most games are over or decided by turn 5 or 6, and so this card is often discard fodder to use for Water Orb or Mirage Frog against most matchups. Barring late game Shao deck mirror play and very situational use vs a potential game-winning Raizan in a Lightning deck, this card has one single purpose in this deck: to bottom deck a Bobu deck’s Stone Masked Ancient. This is just about the only way that this deck can get damage past that 7-cost 3/6 stats entity with Taunt (makes the opponent target that entity).

4. Late Game
Despite this meta’s focus on a strong early and mid game, late game (turn 5+) is still very important for this Shao deck, even if late game against the very prevalent Fire and Lightning deck matchups often only last 1-3 turns. The cards I consider to be the late game closers are Tenshin, Mizuki, and Trade Guild Cavalry. 
Tenshin
Tenshin is a 2-cost weapon that deals 1 damage to a leader On Play, and gives its equipped entity or leader +2 attack. I usually try to stack this for late game so I can finish off an opposing leader’s health late game with one or more Tenshin equips and swings. It’s an unexpected and instant +3 damage (including the ping to leader) that has closed out many games for me.

Mizuki
Mizuki is a 5-cost entity with 2 Gate Power and 3/4 stats. On Play, you can look at the top 3 cards of your deck and pick a 2-cost or less Water card. If that card is an entity, you may play it in the Alley for no additional IKZ cost. Mizuki is the backbone of the Shao deck late game, and the tankiest entity in this deck. Because of this, you can use it to board clear 2-3 attack entities in the opposing Garden with little fear of it being destroyed during the opponents following turn.
Also, it’s almost always most ideal to play Mizuki as soon as you possibly can in order to attack with it sooner, and because of its top deck search card effect to pull a clutch late game (depending on the need):
protective Water Orb or Foamback Crab

the extra needed damage from a Serene Fist Misaki

a searcher (described in the next section) to get a card you need to close out the game.


Trade Guild Cavalry
Trade Guild Cavalry is a 5-cost entity with 2 Gate Power and 3/3 stats. When Attacking, it gives another entity in your Garden +2 attack until the end of that turn. Due to the lack of long late games in most matchups (vs the meta Fire and Lightning decks), I only run 1 because I’m confident that I’ll be able to draw into at least 1-2 of my 5 5-cost entities to play in the late game. After Mizuki, Trade Guild Cavalry is without a doubt the best value entity that costs 5 IKZ.

5. Searchers
I use three types of searcher cards in my deck: Hayabuso Itto, Mizuto, and Tidal Insight. Each serves a different role in acquiring the cards that I need for each game and matchup. The right prioritization of playing these cards can give you the cards you need to win the matchup. Because of this, I run 4 of each of them.
Even if your searches don’t yield anything you want or even applicable cards, they are still useful in getting lower into your shuffled deck and seeing what cards you still have in reach in order to make more informed decisions on what and when to play cards! “Whiffing” your search cards isn’t as bad as it seems!

Hayabusa Itto
Hayabusa Itto is a 1 cost entity with 1 Gate Power and 1/1 stats. It is a searcher that allows you to look at the top 5 cards of your deck and reveal and add 1 Watercrafting-type card to your hand. There are 12 Watercrafting cards in this Shao deck (3 Water Orb, 4 Mirage Frog, 3 Fumiko, 2 Pulled Under), and what you use Hayabusa Itto’s search on depends on your cards in hand and what you’re up against:
Water: Shao mirror matchups are a war of attrition. The biggest damage threats mid-late game tend to be Mizuki and Serene Fist Misaki. Because of the general low damage output per entity on Shao and the advantage on developing board control, you should prioritize getting these Watercrafting cards in your hand, in order of urgency:
1x Fumiko 

1-2x Mirage Frog 

1x Water Orb 

1x Pulled Under 


Lightning: Lightning matchups really on blunting opposing damage, building up board presence, and removing the Silver Current Haruhi and placing your Foamback Crab on board so that you can safely and impactfully use your 1-health entities. If given the choice, you should prioritize getting these Watercrafting cards in your hand, in order of urgency:
1x Fumiko

1x Mirage Frog

2-3x Water Orb


Fire: Fire matchups are similar to Lightning matchups but even more heavily rely on delaying opposing damage and protecting against big 4-5 damage attacks to your leader health. If given the choice, you should prioritize getting these Watercrafting cards in your hand, in order of urgency:
1-2x Mirage Frog

1-2x Water Orb

1x Fumiko


Earth: Earth matchups almost always hinge on your ability to neutralize the opposing Stone Masked Ancients, and to protect your Mizuki late game from getting into Quicksand’s 2-health affecting range. If given the choice, you should prioritize getting these Watercrafting cards in your hand, in order of urgency:
2x Pulled Under (prioritize this above all else!)

2x Water Orb

1-2x Mirage Frog



Mizuto
Mizuto is a 1 cost entity with 1 Gate Power and 1/1 stats. It is a searcher that allows you to look at the top 5 cards of your deck and reveal and add 1 Driftward-type card to your hand. There are 8 Driftward cards in this Shao deck (4 Serene Fist Misaki, 4 Foamback Crabs), and what you use Mizuto’s search on depends on your cards in hand and what you’re up against:
Water: Water mirrors usually don’t have a lot of big attackers, so you tend to want to stack your own big attackers as much as possible. If given the choice, you should prioritize getting these Driftward cards in your hand, in order of urgency:
2x Serene Fist Misaski

1x Foamback Crab

2x Serene Fist Misaki


Lightning: Against Lightning decks, you definitely want to turtle a bit while developing your board and surviving until your 4-5 cost entities on turns 5+. If given the choice, you should prioritize getting these Driftward cards in your hand, in order of urgency:
1x Foamback Crab

1x Serene Fist Misaki

1-2x Foamback Crab

2x Serene Fist Misaki


Fire: The Fire matchup really boils down to getting a Defender on the board to scare off the potential Tenmoku Daiki play on turn 4, but flooding the board with as much damage as possible to outscale Fire’s damage later in the game. If given the choice, you should prioritize getting these Driftward cards in your hand, in order of urgency:
1x Foamback Crab

2-3x Serene Fist Misaki

1x Foamback Crab


Earth: Going against Earth decks tend not to need much Driftward cards early game, especially since Quicksand destroys all of this deck’s Driftward cards. That being said, it’s still good to build up your board or keep cards in hand to rebuild your board post-Quicksand. If given the choice, you should prioritize getting these Driftward cards in your hand, in order of urgency:
1x Foamback Crab

2-3x Serene Fist Misaki

2x Foamback Crab



Tidal Insight
Tidal Insight is a 1-cost spell that allows you to look at the top 3 cards of your deck, add a Water card to your hand and then top- or bottom-deck the other two cards in any order. Because of its ability to effectively acquire any Water card from the top 3 cards, and manipulate the next 2 in any ways, it is the single best Searcher card in the game. 
It is always beneficial to use last IKZ in a turn on Tidal Insight as soon as possible to help set up your hand and board with the cards that you want over the next few turns. Tidal Insight’s utility has probably won me more games than any other card in this deck.

Tips and Tricks
Above, I’ve described every single card in my Shao deck. If you’ve made it this far, congrats: with practice and repetition, you should be able to play it well. The following tips and tricks help make that trial-and-error period that comes with learning any new deck a little easier.

When to Mulligan
When you first draw 7 cards for your starting hand, you and your opponent are offered the chance to “mulligan”, or redraw your hand. The general rule of thumb is to mulligan if you can’t see a clear and strong turns 1 and 2 for your matchup. The most ideal start for this Shao deck is 1-2 Hayabusa Itto or Mizuto, Tidal Insight, 1-2 Driftward entity, 1-2 Watercrafting entity, a 4- or 5-cost entity, and a spell or Tenshin or two.
Here are a few examples of 7-card hands, and early game notes on each one:
Hand #1: Very solid draw, especially against Fire decks with the 2 unpingable Serene Fist Misakis, and of course the strong Hayabusa Itto (+portal into the Garden for 1 IKZ back) + Tidal Insight first turn. If my turn 1 Searchers can’t get me a Fumiko and/or Mirage Frog, I’d probably play this hand more aggressively and try to get both of my Serene Fist Misaki on the board by turn 3 so that I can quickly ramp up damage instead of attempting to slow down the opposing damage.
Hand #2: Another great draw. I’d probably look to use my turn 1 to play either the Mizuto or Hayabusa Itto (depending on the matchup) + Tidal Insight, and then try to play my Fumiko(s) as quickly as I can. To do so, I’d have to make sure that I protect my Mizuto/Hayabusa Itto from getting attacked, and so I likely either wouldn’t attack with them if the opponent may have charged entities the following turn, or leave 1 IKZ up to use Shao ability. This hand is likely going to allow me to play more control, with the Fumiko allowing me to flood my Garden with entities faster.
Hand #3: At first glance, this is a very awful hand. Not much portal value to get back IKZ every turn, and barely any entities. Against most decks, you would be correct in wanting to mulligan this hand. However, this starting hand is actually pure gold against an Earth deck. You have a Mizuryuu’s Torret for Earth defenders, and already a Pulled Under in hand for a Stone Masked Ancient. Combined with the Hayabusa Itto to search for your second Pulled Under, and the Trade Guild Cavalry + Tenshin for the late game showdown, this is a nearly perfect hand against an Earth deck.

*Note: if I see that I have 2 Young Shaos or 2 Mizuki while going second, I think about a gameplay flow where I hold off on using my IKZ token to play the Young Shao or Mizuki as early as possible. This way, my board develops its mid-late game entities as quickly as possible.

When to Use Shao’s Leader Ability
Using Shao’s Leader Ability can be difficult because you’re effectively leaving 1 IKZ up at the end of the turn in hopes that you’re getting more value from reducing an opponent’s attack by 1 than playing an additional card.
For early game, I tend to keep 1 IKZ untapped for my Shao Leader Ability in just about every turn from turns 1-3 vs more aggressive decks (Lightning, Fire, or another aggro Water deck), unless I can play a Tidal Insight. Reason being, I need to hold off the heavier early game damage from these two decks attacking my Leader’s health. The only exception to this is if I draw into more aggressive early game damage against Fire decks (more Misaki, more 1-cost 1/1 stat cards, Tenshins, etc): in this situation, I’ll play more aggressively since I’ll be able to match the Fire deck’s early game more effectively by using my IKZ to play the additional entities or weapons.
Against Earth decks, I tend to be more liberal with my IKZ usage every turn because they very rarely deal much damage until they’ve ramped up to play their 6-8 cost cards, and I tend not to leave one up for defense.
During mid-game and late-game, I tend to use my Shao Leader ability to protect certain high-damage output entities in my Shao kit, such as Serene Fist Misaki, Young Shao, and Mizuki. Because these cards have higher attack, if I can use Shao ability to defend an attack to one of those, I can usually remove one more entity from the opposing board trying to take me out, thus accelerating my Garden development over theirs.
A Few Things to Look Out For
Every element poses different threats for this Shao deck kit. You don’t need to read all this as most of it just comes with practice. But if you want to have a shortcut to some of the info gleaned from practice, bear with me here:
Water:
Closely monitor changes in health of any Young Shaos on the board as you and your opponent battle for board control. I’ve won several games against Shao decks by simply taking advantage of the fact that if I can either turn their 3/3 Young Shao into a 2/2 during my turn and then making it easier to take out.

Don’t overuse Mirage Frog’s card effect! Both Mirage Frog and Water Orb require you to discard a card to use its effect, and with a deck where you can regain IKZ to use every turn, a diminished number of cards in hand can be a very real problem late game. Usually using the Mirage Frog ability once or twice is enough before you start hurting your own gameplay.

When a Water deck player has 1, 2, or 3 IKZ untapped by the end of their turn, always think through all the response card scenarios. Between Water Orbs, Shao Leader Ability, Commune with Water, Chilling Water, and Rippling Recalls, there are a lot of disruptive response cards an opposing Water player can use to get an advantage during your turn. Be mindful of those!


Lightning:
Make sure to understand during every turn that unlike most decks, Lightning’s kit has so many ways that they can deal damage to your Leader and entities without having to wait for a turn to charge. Because of this, always be prepared for the worst - stacking of weapons, charged Silver Current Haruhi/Black Jade Crewleader, Indra, etc. Fortunately as it stands, there’s only so many entities and weapons that just about all Lightning deck users use, and this familiarity just comes with practice.

When a Lightning deck player has 1 or 2 IKZ untapped by the end of their turn, always think through all the response card scenarios. Lightning Orbs and Hidden Daggers for instance can take down entities you want to keep alive, such as Serene Fist Misaki or a Hayabusa Itto/Mizuto that you were planning on using for the Fumiko effect next turn. Remember: if you don’t attack, they can’t respond. 

Always anticipate a turn 2 or 3 Silver Current Haruhi, and adjust your Fumiko combo accordingly. A charged Silver Current Haruhi can completely derail your Fumiko play by pinging an untapped Hayabuso Itto/Mizuto.

Do NOT use Mirage Frog’s card effect to bounce an Alpine Prowler back to the opponents hand unless you want to be hit by a very inexpensive Tenshin swing via the Surge Gate ability.


Fire:
Fire is a tough matchup especially in the current meta of focusing on stacking the deck with the many 1- and 2- cost spells and entities that can do 2-3 damage per swing. Do not be afraid to be aggressive and swing to face instead of board clearing, unless you can take out an entity like Black Jade Warlord or Enraged Howler, whose card effects enable them to deal even more damage.

Be aware of your health when you’re approaching 7-health or 5-health. Scarlett does 7 unblockable damage as a 5-cost entity, and Fire Orb does 5 unblockable damage as a 4-cost spell. If you survive even later into the game, look out for the Collateral Burst/Detonation Pact damage on top of those. 

Because the current Fire card pool doesn’t have any direct response cards, all you really need to worry about when attacking is not overextending and leaving cards like Serene Fist Misaki and Young Shao vulnerable to getting taken down by the many low-cost 2-attack cards in the Fire deck. Keep that last IKZ up at the end of your turn sometimes!


Earth:
As I mentioned above, your entire game plan vs the Earth deck is to find your copies of Pulled Under and Mizuryuu’s Torrent and hope that the opponent doesn’t find more Stone Masked Ancients, Geodust Smugglers, Sandcoil Pythons, and Teb Feas. Water vs Earth is always going to be an uphill battle because of the way Earth decks ramp up and can defend in the process.

If your opponent starts randomly attacking your Mizuki or Trade Guild Cavalry late game with 1- or 2-attack entities, beware of the Quicksand. When your tankier entities go down to 2-health, Quicksand will include them in the board wipe. Make sure you defend around that!

Try to keep your Foamback Crab(s) in the Alley until you actually need them to block damage late game. This will protect them against any Quicksands that occur before late game, and this strategy has bought me the needed extra turns to win this matchup late game multiple times.

There are many times where I don’t actually attack with my entities until late game, and just spend my turns building my board up. The reason for this is that often times, a good Earth deck user will portal in a 1/1 entity as a defender or use their Bobu Leader Ability that heals their Leader by 1 if an entity is destroyed. So not only will the entity I attack with gets damaged (and might get taken down with help from an Earth Orb), but the opponent heals from that Bobu Leader Ability.


*Important Note: I’d say the most important differentiator between a good player and a bad player is recognizing when to attack and when not to attack. As you keep playing against different matchups, you’ll start noticing situations where it’s just better not to attack to prevent a response card or effect that might swing the momentum of the game against you.

=== azuki-tech-talk-ed | Azuki Tech Talk | Ed
Ed
Ed gives us a look at the indomitable human I mean bean spirit, his flavor text (usually a snippet of perspective from the character’s view at the bottom of the card) allows us to have some insight as to what Ed will be in your deck.
Above all else growing pains are needed to get better in Azuki TCG and finding tech that works for you is part of that, some tech works better for some than others and the only way to find out is to test and run into as many situational occurrences as you can.
Why
Ed provides us with an alternative option to [Young Shao] who normally takes the place of our 4 cost entities in water decks.
Do you want another option that requires thought and effort to remove without having to consider too many other things to keep it safe  
Then Ed is for you, its 3/3 body will keep it safe from high risk situational cards from all decks like [Quicksand], [Collateral Burst], and [Thunderclap] multiple [Hidden Dagger]/[Lightning Orb].
When
Ed being a 4 cost takes some of the mental energy of when to play it out of the consideration. Playing Ed turn 4 against any deck will require adjustment from your opponent a 3/3 body that isn’t easily removed isn’t something that can be ignored for long as water begins its ramp in [Mizuki] for turn 5. 
Making your opponent waste resources on a pseudo boss monster is important especially turn 4 when most elements are getting their tools to either ramp / provide more protection, direct damage from spells and even board wipes.
Ed simply is the play for turn 4 and any turn after that if Mizuki is not available.
How
Playing Ed is simple, he comes gated up turn 4 with 1 IKZ restanded thanks to [Hydromancy Gate].
Some options with the left over IKZ can either be to save it for protection with Shao’s (leader) ability, another response or summon another low cost entity.
Moving forward from turn 4 Ed becomes almost impossible to remove as he is pay walled behind [Shao] and [Foamback Crab]. Essentially requiring your opponent to waste sources in an effort to swing over something that can be protected with minimal resources and its own big body.

40 of the Top 64 decks ran Zero (~63%)

21 of the Top 32 decks ran Zero (~66%)

6 of the Top 8 decks ran Zero with Rushfire Gate (75% of the Top Cut)

All 4 Top 4 decks ran Zero with Rushfire Gate

With numbers like that, the format had effectively narrowed to one deck. Players either ran it or built specifically to beat it.
The case against Rushfire specifically
The Zero/Rushfire pairing posted the highest playrate and Top 32 presence of anything at the regional, making it the clearest target. Other overperforming pieces, like the Blackjade Warlord, were considered. The team concluded those cards were only as strong as they were because Rushfire Gate enabled them. Cutting the gate addresses the root cause rather than trimming symptoms.
There's also a design concern going forward. As long as Rushfire Gate exists, every small 1-, 2-, or 3-drop Fire or Neutral card has to be checked against whether it becomes broken when paired with rush or sacrifice effects, a constraint that was starting to box in future card design.
The other Fire gate, Ragefire Gate, performed well at the Invitational before Rushfire's release and is still considered a strong, competitive card. The team expects Fire to remain a top-tier archetype even with Rushfire gone.
The path back for Rushfire
Banned cards aren't necessarily banned forever, but Rushfire Gate won't be reconsidered until the team is confident other decks have a real answer to the early-game pressure it creates. Because physical card games can't be patched like live-service titles, Chao's team says it will keep leaning on close meta monitoring, both quantitative and qualitative, to decide on future bans, unbans, and errata, while trying to keep those interventions rare.
The team's goal is a healthier format with three or four competitive decks, not a single deck crowding out everything else. The team says it's looking forward to seeing what new deck archetypes emerge for the rest of Set 1 and the regionals still ahead.
— Based on remarks from Jon Chao, Game Director, Azuki TCG

=== azuki-tech-talk-stalking-assassin | Azuki Tech Talk | Water Tech
Stalking Assassin
[Stalking Assassin] in Water ([Shao] /[Hydromancy Gate (Alt Art)]) feels like it might be something you’d pass off, as it forces you into a decision to only attack your opponent's leader.
A simple decision to only swing has created a paradigm which is almost impossible to ignore with the pure force that Water is able to flood with. If you peek closer at its stats, you’ll notice that Stalking Assassin has 2 gate power for a 3 cost in a deck where [Fumiko] is almost expected turn 3. Stalking Assassin allows you to throw your opponents into the realm of bracing for an entity that not only is beefed up at 2/2 stats and 2 gate power, but creates a board state that they now must prepare for long term.
Giving yourself back 2 IKZ (Shao ability) allows the deck to now operate with other options moving into your opponent's turn ([Sundering Strike], [Mocking Dummy], extra entity summon). Water now has another tool to turn the tides early on and create a situation that is much more complicated for your opponent to deal with.
Stalking Assassin is the Knight in Shining Armor of a Water deck because turn 3 post-[Rushfire Gate] ban now creates the momentum for the rest of the game. Turn 3, many opponents begin to expend resources to remove board states and attempt to take control. This forces opponents to expend energy on Stalking Assassin, who isn’t a primary member of our team but does require attention and proper removal or will chip away at health and create a bigger barrier for opponents later on.
The limited viability of Stalking Assassin isn’t felt early, as the only Taunt ability in the game is almost 4 more turns away ([Stone Masked Ancient]). It also provides some complexity so that opponents who might only expect you to end turn 3 with 1 IKZ after playing Fumiko now have to deal with Stalking Assassin and 2 restanded IKZ. You’ve now created a more complex board for your opponent to overcome.
Water doesn’t feel like it is limited to its similar path through turns 1, 2, and 3 anymore, as it’s created variability in a game where predictability can mean the difference between a loss and a win.
Why
Stalking Assassin provides us with an alternative option to Fumiko, who normally takes the place of our 3-cost entities in Water decks and turn 3 overall. It creates varied situations for your opponents to consider. Similarly, it creates a board state in which you are now more equipped to respond to threats moving forward.
When
Stalking Assassin being a 3 cost and limited to attacking leaders creates the situation itself where it wants to be played as early as possible. Using token early on turn 2 is always an option. Swing as much as possible before turn 7 against the only Taunt in the game (Stone Masked Ancient).
Playing Stalking Assassin turn 2 with token or turn 3 are both viable plays and will create stronger boards in the long run due to its strong gate power and healthy attack and defensive stats.
How
Playing Stalking Assassin is simple. She comes gated up turn 2 or 3, depending on if you have token, with 2 IKZ restanded thanks to Hydromancy Gate.
Although the forced condition to attack leader can feel restrictive, it isn’t really due to other options in Water that allow for board wipes ([Mizuryuu's Torrent], [Pulled Under]), as well as smaller entities that can flood the board and push and swing into other bodies ([Hayabusa Itto], [Hayabusa Saburo], [Mirage Frog], [Benzai the Merchant]).
Otherwise, you’ve placed a 3-cost entity with 2 gate power and a 2/2 body, which is pretty heavy at the current game state of Azuki. Opponents will need to consider the cost of swinging into her and possibly losing a body or trading with a counter like Sundering Strike. Supported by Shao and Mocking Dummy, Stalking Assassin becomes difficult to remove and a pest for opponents due to its need to swing into leader for 2 damage every turn.

=== goros-gravelly-grit | Goro’s Gravelly Grit | Are You A Beanliever?
It’s a Wednesday night.
The wife and kids are sound asleep and I’m sitting at my kitchen table with Azuki TCG cards strewn across it. 
I just got Exodia Obliterated at yet another Genki local.
It’s quiet.
But not too quiet.

And my rank for Regionals:
Against The Grain
As a wise old man named ExpectedValue once told me, “Every card has a design bias” or a natural inclination towards a certain play style. Sometimes it is intentional, sometimes not.
At the time, it seemed like such an obvious, dumb thing to say. However, thinking about the cards from this perspective can help determine and react to which tendencies align, conflict and disrupt.
The Earth element as a whole has a design bias that encourages Earth players to “survive” until the end game. 
“It’s a marathon, not a sprint.”
Because of this, a lot of people, myself included, questioned if Goro was even a viable enough option to make competitive. After all, if it’s endgame survival we’re talking about, Bobu does the same thing, but better.
So, for me, the question that needed to be answered was: How do I make a deck that values Goro more than Bobu? 
In order to do this, I had to think completely outside of Earth’s design bias. 
Flip the Script
From my perspective, the gap in the meta was that all of these elements leaned into their inherent design tendencies (as they should). This meant two things off the bat:
Most of the players at Regionals would run Fire decks because, by design, it is the most efficient at winning, in general.

Everyone is expecting each element to play a certain way and at a certain tempo.

As a long-time Bobu player, I had to switch my mindset on there being this singular, correct way to pilot Earth. 
There had to be another way.
I mean, it was either that or I completely miss the mark on having the element of surprise and get Exodia Obliterated yet again. 
To start off, I had to front-load the deck so that most of the value I got was during the early to mid-game turns instead of late game like most Earth decks are accustomed to doing. 
Now circling back to my little bean friend, Jay. 
Focusing on building the deck to revolve around Jay and the BEANZ archetype not only aligned perfectly with the switch in play style, since it gave me the early game relevance that I needed to make this concept work, but it also aligned perfectly with Goro’s design since Goro is able to interact with neutral entities (unlike Bobu).
The core concept was there, now all I had to do was Tetris the last pieces so that it maximized the focal point of the deck’s strategy. 
Breaking It Down - The Tetris Pieces
[Offering to Stillstone] - Because there are so many cards in the deck that are included for specific match ups, there are naturally dead cards in hand that can be discarded. Stillstone gives my deck the flexibility to ramp into an early Jay or an early Quicksand to catch my opponent off guard. [Healing Flutter] - Predicting a majority of Fire deck users at Regionals meant 4 copies of Healing Flutter was an absolute must. [Sundering Strike] - As I’ve said before, Fire is a problem, and I can’t just go around leaving myself exposed to the elements when one of those elements is a Black Jade Warlord with Charge. [Link] - While I do think that Jay is the best card in this specific deck, I do think that Link is the best overall BEANZ card at the moment, maybe forever. [Mocking Dummy] - Including 4 copies of this card into my deck was a recent change. [Rei] - My favorite non-BEANZ neutral entity in the game right now. As a 1 cost entity with 1 attack and 2 health, it’s super cost efficient and a very sticky entity, which translates great going into the more fast match ups against Lightning and Fire.

The Match Ups - Risk Versus Reward
Risk and Reward is at the heart of any competitive TCG.
What are the potential risks and the potential payoffs for running a certain deck and/or certain cards? 
Which decks are you predicting that you’ll run into? 
How are you predicting those decks to be run?
Will my opponent wear deodorant?
I don’t know. No one actually KNOWS.
For the most part, the deck that you make is going to be strong against some decks and weak against others. 
For this BEANZ deck, the match ups actually aligned pretty well with many others and I predicted to be a faster, more aggressive meta.
Overall, this deck does better against aggro builds and is less effective against control-centric builds.
Water
This is my worst match up, and by a very large margin. 
Two words that make this match absolutely abysmal: Mizuryuu’s. Torrent. 
I know what you’re thinking. 
“BuT yOu HaVE QuiCksaNd!”
In this and my Earth match ups are where Quicksand gives me the most value yes, however, against water, it only gets rid of the smaller, less threatening entities (Fumiko, Benzai, Mirage Frog, etc.) While this IS good, all of these entities have On Play effects, which means that by the time I’ve used Quicksand, these slippery little bastards have already gotten value from being played.
Luckily, I only saw a single water match up in my Regionals experience, but it was during my 7th game and I got absolutely rinsed (no pun intended). 
Earth
If I had run into any Goro with Devotion Gate decks, that probably would be my worse match up as there’s really nothing I can do about the low cost removal.
But luckily, I only ran into one traditionally built Bobu with Stonehaven Gate deck during my second round.
In this match up, Quicksand is actually my best friend whether I have it in hand or my opponent has it in hand. 
All of my beefy entities are not affected by Quicksand, so it’s a dead card in their hand. Jay, Ed, Prickly Tumbleweed, Gin and Tonika all immune to Quicksand.
All of their entities that they want to be “sticky” are all susceptible to Quicksand. Kale, Shroom Tender, Warding Totem. And in order for Stonemask Ancient to be fully effective, it needs a Defender out. Otherwise, I’m just Goro buffing the Ed and killing it with Ed and Gin and Tonika attacks.
But, if I do NOT draw into a Quicksand, the battle does get a lot tougher, but not impossible.
Lightning
Lightning is formidable, yes. But this was probably my easiest match up of the 4 elements. Especially considering that the Lightning players that I faced have never had to play against a wave of Jay and Shroom Tender Defenders, it left them with very awkward decisions to make.
Lightning gets most, if not all, of its value from attacking. There aren’t many cards for this element that generate damage without attacking, so trying to get rid of my Jay and/or Shroom Tender Defenders becomes a game of chicken. 
Will I let an attack go through or will I allow the trade to happen?
If my opponent commits too much to an attack to bait out my Defender, chances are, I have another Jay or Shroom Tender in my hand and was able to get more value from that trade.
If my opponent tries to bait out my Defender but doesn’t commit enough for me to get a favorable trade, and I allow the attack through, then I’m still getting more value out of my Defender over time.
I ended up playing 2 Lightning decks at the Regionals, both of which I was able to squeeze out the W from. 
Fire
This element is both the reason why I even made this deck in the first place, and it’s my most stressful match up.
Unfortunately, Fire does what it does really well and very efficiently. 
There is no single line to victory against Fire, especially with Rushfire Gate, and the margin of error is super small because a lot of my win condition is based off of forcing my opponent to make unfavorable decisions.
But, in a nutshell, I basically want to have enough Jays, Shroom Tenders, Healing Flutters and Sundering Strikes to outresource my opponent so that I can hit them with lethal damage before they end me.
Super fast-paced, and always stressful. Any 1 move could be the deciding factor.
I ended up playing against 4 Fire decks at Regionals and losing to 1 of them. Shoutout to my brother, ZzBlaze. 
Last Thoughts
All games fundamentally aim to frustrate their players. 
TCGs take that frustration, double it, and give it to the same person, and all persons that partake thereafter. It’s not just the opponent that you’re playing against, you are playing against the deckbuilding process itself. 
This is why the payoff in TCGs hits so different when a deck that you built from scratch performs.
Going into Regionals, I heard “this won’t work”, “it’s bad”, “just play Bobu, it’s better” etc. I’m not gonna lie, there were days I believed it as well. 
But it worked.
And I think how the deck performed says more than just the result itself and it bodes well for the future of the Azuki TCG, in my opinion.
It shows that Set 1 still has layers to it that still haven’t been fully explored.
It shows that the game’s current design has enough healthy variance built in to reward a good, unconventional thesis.
If you’re like me, the type of player who lives for making unconventional ideas work, then pursue that. 
For me, it was never just about winning and losing, it was and is the only way that I’ve ever been able to experience a TCG fully.

=== aez-s-shao-deck-guide-q2-2026 | Aez's Shao Deck Guide, Q2 2026 | A breakdown of the deck that brought me to #1 on Azuki TCG's leaderboards.
Author’s Notes:
This is the deck I used to grind to Rank #1 on the Azuki TCG leaderboard. I personally spent hundreds of hours iterating and reiterating on playstyle and build, tinkering with ratios and matchups until I arrived at this version of the deck. While it’s worth noting the fact that I don’t use this exact deck anymore since the Rushfire Gate ban, I hope that many of the concepts and build explanations I include in this article will help readers make informed decisions on how to play and build a proper Shao deck. I will still include many of the thoughts I had while building this deck, with Rushfire in mind.
If you’ve read my previous Shao deck article, you’ll notice that the structure of this one will be a little different. This will not be another 6000-word breakdown of my deck. I received feedback from awesome readers that while very informative, the previous article felt a little bit overwhelming. I would recommend first reading through each card to understand their effects in order to better grasp the concepts outlined here. 
In this article, I will be going turn-by-turn, describing ideal turns, and things to look out for at each step and against each deck so that someone can follow this deck guide with more linear flow within the gameplay. Hope you enjoy!


Deck Build


Overview
In general, this deck is built to maximize card draw without giving up too much tempo. Shao/Hydromancy Gate decks are some of the most flexible in the game, but also one of the most inconsistent in its current format. Therefore, you want to:
Use your low cost entities (Rei, Mocking Dummy) and searchers/drawers (Hayabusa Itto, Mizuto, Benzai, Hayabusa Saburo) to flood the board as much as possible while searching for what you need mid-late game to win.

Use Mirage Frog to build tempo, bouncing 2-health entities and late game defenders back to opponents’ hands. You may also use it to bounce searchers/drawers back to your hand in order to replay them, as a last resort.

Use the Fumiko to build tempo, bouncing 1-cost entities back to your hand and play 2-cost entities, while also using it to play or replay low cost searchers and drawers where needed

Use Mizuto as much as possible to get Foamback Crabs into your hand vs Raizan leader decks. That is your main win condition - Fumiko and replay it where you can.

Keep 1 IKZ up vs Rushfire Gate decks when they have enough IKZ (3 IKZ) to use Pekiro/Cinderwake Ritualist to gate in Black Jade Warlord, in order to use or threaten the Sundering Strike

Be constantly aware of where you aim to use Sundering Strike. Vs Earth, maybe it’s using it vs Sandcoil Python when they swing into your Mizuryuu Fist Master. Vs Lightning, maybe it’s using it on Rooftop Hunter, on Silver Hand Haruhi when they swing into your Rei, or on Mo when they swing into your Mizuki. Vs Water, maybe it’s using it vs an opposing Mizuki when they swing into your own Mizuki. Think of all the possibilities when you have Sundering Strike in hand - be creative, and don’t waste it!

Vs non-Raizan leader matchups, stack your Foamback Crabs in the alley where they can’t be removed by most typical decks, and only bring them out when needed. (play them directly into the Garden vs Raizan decks, with its plethora of charged entities)

Use your bottom-deck cards (Mizuryuu’s Torrent, Pulled Under, Mizuryuu Fist Master) wisely. You have a limited amount, so maximize their use - only bottom-deck opponent cards that you think will be an ongoing problem for you to deal with, or if you can swing to end game by doing so.

Keep an eye out for opposing response cards - if they have IKZ available, always assume they have the necessary spells or responses to use against you. Don’t take unnecessary risks (like attacking with Hayabusa Itto before you want to use Fumiko on it, with opposing 1 IKZ available for use)

Don’t immediately use Soryu No Rin on turn 8; sometimes it’s better not to play it when you have a valuable hand that will allow you to swing for lethal soon, or if the opponent has Teb Fea on board. Always think about your win condition and your opponent’s win condition to make an informed game decision.

Don’t use Aquatic Veil just to randomly prevent 3 damage. Surprise damage reduction is priceless in this deck. Use it to save an important entity from a big swing, or to prevent lethal damage the next turn. Think of Aquatic Veil almost as a surprise Foamback Crab in this deck.

Don’t use Tenshin just to use 2 IKZ. Have a purpose when you use your Tenshins - you only have 4. Finish off a big opposing entity. Destroy an opposing defender. Stack them and swing for late game lethal. NEVER waste your Tenshins - just like with Aquatic Veil, surprise damage is priceless. If you get to a point where your opponent always thinks you have Tenshins in hand, you’ve already won half of the mental game.



Turn-By-Turn Breakdown
This is a rough breakdown of what I think about when I play my deck, turn-by-turn, vs every matchup.

Mulligan Decision
One of your most important decisions in game is going to be: “should I mulligan this starting hand?” With this deck, there are a lot of nuances based on matchup, but it’s ultimately up to your playstyle and deck consistency. As with any other deck, however, you’ll want to see a clear turns 1-4 in your starting hand. If you don’t see the potential to use up your IKZ in your first 4 turns, you’ll want to mulligan.
If you’re going second, keep in mind what turn you want to use your IKZ token - you are basically playing a specific turn twice (I.E. On Turn 4 you can play 5 (4 IKZ + 1 Token), and on Turn 5 you can play 5 IKZ again).

Vs. Water (Shao/Hydromancy):
since games tend to go longer, you want to control the board as much as possible

your ideal starting hand has clear turns 1-5, with a Mizuki in hand

Prioritize Itto; you want to get your Mizuryuu Fist Master, Pulled Under, and Soryu No Rin in hand to dominate the late game.

Prioritize Mizuryuu’s Torrent; you want to bottom deck their Mizuki to win the mid-game

Hand 1.1)
Mulligan this hand (Hand 1.1) vs water. It’s good that there’s a Mizuki and Soryu No Rin, but theres very little you can play from turns 3-4, and your searcher (Mizuto) doesn’t search anything useful for you in this matchup.

Hand 1.2)
Keep this hand (Hand 1.2) vs water. You have the Mizuryuu’s Torrent and Mizuki to compete in the mid-game. You have bodies to play in the early game with Hayabusa Itto and Hayabusa Saburo. You have Fumiko to bounce and replay the Hayabusa Saburo to find other late-game options in the mirror.
Vs. Earth (Bobu or Goro/Stonehaven):
your goal in this matchup is to deal as much damage as possible, as quickly as possible (be ready for the Turn 5 Quicksand!), while stacking up on your tankier late game options

your ideal starting hand should be very similar vs Earth as it is vs Water

in this matchup, it’s more important to stack the alley with Foamback Crabs so that you can prevent Teb Fea and Sandcoil Python from swinging into your Mizuki and Mizuryuu Fist Master before they can win you the game

more priority on having Pulled Under in your hand to deal with Stone Masked Ancient

Vs. Fire (Zero/Rushfire):
this will be your fastest and worst matchup; you want a bunch of low cost entities to flood the board as much as possible. Your game will not last longer than 5-6 turns in 95% of these matchups- plan accordingly

you want at least 1 Sundering Strike in your hand, in order to deal with Black Jade Warlord.

having a Mizuryuu’s Torrent for turn 4 to remove a Pekiro/Cinderwake Ritualist + a 1- or 2- cost entity is a bonus


Hand 2.1)
Mulligan this hand (Hand 2.1) vs fire. Against earth, this would be a borderline keepable hand, but vs fire, it’s just too many high-cost cards that don’t flood your board quickly enough. 5 out of the 7 cards are unplayable before turn 4.

Hand 2.2)
Keep this hand (Hand 2.2) vs fire. You have 4 different cards that you can play on turn 1-2, you have a Mizuryuu’s Torrent for the turn 4 removal, and you have a Sundering Strike for the rushed Turn 3 Black Jade Warlord.
Vs. Lightning (Raizan/Surge)
your goal is to prolong the game so that building your board will eventually out-damage the opposing 4/5-damage swings after turn 4.

with this in mind, you want to flood board with entities, and you want as many Foamback Crabs as you can get


Hand 3.1)
Mulligan this hand (Hand 3.1) vs lightning. Even though double Sundering Strike is a great way to take care of the dreaded turn 4 Black Jade Crewleader swing for 5 damage, you ultimately want Foamback Crabs and more entities in your hand. Dealing consistent damage to board or leader from turns 2-4 is crucial while you ramp up your damage.

Hand 3.2)
Keep this hand (Hand 3.2) vs lightning. You have multiple low-cost entities you can flood the board with early game to ramp up your damage quickly, and you have Mizuto with the Fumiko, which will allow you to search for a Foamback Crab at least 10 cards deep into the deck, which gives you breathing room for the heavy Turn 4 Black Jade Crewleader or Stalking Assassin swing.

Turn 1
Because this deck has 14 playable 1-cost entities, you should be able to play something on Turn 1 pretty consistently
if you can play an entity in the alley and use your gate to restand 1 IKZ, always do so

if you can play a second entity with your restanded IKZ, always do so as well



Turn 2
Use turn 2 to continue building tempo. You ideally want to play more 1-cost entities, and/or play a Mirage Frog to bounce opposing entities back to their hand.
vs water, leave 1 IKZ up to protect your 1 health entity if the opponent has a Rei or Mirage Frog on board and can otherwise take out your Hayabusa Itto/Mizuto without being destroyed. You want to maintain board control.

vs earth, don’t risk swinging your Hayabusa Itto/Mizuto into their 1-cost defender if you’re planning on playing Fumiko next turn in order to bounce it back to your hand. Always assume that they will block and take that trade.

vs fire, try to use Mirage Frog to bounce an opposing Rei or Kindler or Seer if you can, just to delay its damage for one more turn - one damage can be the difference between win and loss. This is where you want to keep 1 IKZ up if you went first, to threaten the Sundering Strike and prevent a Pekiro-Black Jade Warlord combo on your oppoenent’s next turn.

vs lightning, continue to flood board, and feel free to trade your 1-cost entity into theirs if you can; the following turn, your 1-health entity will likely be destroyed by a Silver Current Haruhi, so you can be more free with that entity.



Turn 3
Ideally, you want to use your turn 3 to play the Fumiko in the alley, bounce a 1-cost entity back to your hand, and play a 2-cost entity (Benzai, Mirage Frog, Foamback Crab) into the garden. You get a 1-IKZ advantage in this way, and you can continue to build tempo. You can also play a Hayabusa Saburo into the garden after portalling in the Fumiko to draw another card.
vs water, you’re going to want to remove the opposing board as much as possible with your 2-health entities: this is the battle of board control.

vs earth, you may want to bounce your 1-cost entity with the Fumiko and play a Foamback Crab in the alley. This prevents your opponent from getting additional value from Quicksand if they use it on turn 5 to wipe your board. Remember: Quicksand can destroy Foamback Crabs.

vs fire, from this point on, you’re going to want to continue to keep 1 IKZ available to use Sundering Strike on the opponent’s rushed Black Jade Warlord. Even if you don’t have it, it’s worth making the opponent rethink playing it this turn. Also, if you use Fumiko to play an entity with 1 gate power, play it into the alley. This allows you to use Mizuryuu’s Torrent on your next turn, then gate in that entity to keep 1 IKZ available for the Sundering Strike on the opponent’s next turn. Always think ahead.

vs lightning, this is where you want to start thinking about having a Foamback Crab in the garden. If lightning has access to 4 IKZ, it’s very common for them to play and charge Black Jade Crewleader or Stalking Assassin for 4-5 damage- using Foamback Crab to block it is usually pretty crucial to winning the matchup. If the opponent has a tapped Silver Current Haruhi on board, I may even consider using Tenshin to attach to my leader and swinging into it to take it out.



Turn 4
On Turn 4, Mizuryuu’s Torrent is generally the best move. Removing 2 slightly tankier entities from the opposing board while still  being able to swing with the board that you’ve been building is crucial for starting to swing the game momentum back into your favor. If you don’t have a Mizuryuu’s Torrent in hand, continuing to build board with another Fumiko or other entities is almost never a bad play.
vs water, I would save my Mizuryuu’s Torrent solely to use for an opposing Mizuki, unless you have 2 or more in hand. 

vs earth, I would use my Mizuryuu’s Torrent against a defending Kale + another entity, or a Warding Totem + a 1-cost entity. This is a crucial turn for you to deal damage with your board before they can afford the IKZ to Quicksand and clear your board.

vs fire, use your Mizuryuu’s Torrent vs a 3-cost entity and a Rei/Fanatic Kindler/Cinderwake Seer. Make sure you portal in the 1-gate power you played on Turn 3 in order to keep 1 IKZ up to threaten the Sundering Strike.

vs lightning, continue to build board and keep a Foamback Crab in the garden if you can. If you don’t have one there, try to search for one using Mizuto or using Fumiko to bounce and replay a Mizuto or Benzai to find a Foamback Crab to play.



Turn 5 and onward
Your best move on Turn 5 and onward will almost always be developing your board by playing the most tanky entity you are able to play- either Mizuki or Mizuryuu Fist Master. After turn 5, you’ll start looking for your win conditions and prepping to prevent your opponents’ win conditions. 
vs water, just try to out-tempo your opponent. Clear their board, and swing to opponent lead. Use Tenshin to take out opposing Mizuki and Mizuryuu Fist Master by surprise, and look for a good opportunity to drop Soryu No Rin. This matchup is probably the most ideal matchup to play the Soryu No Rin. 

vs earth, it’s always an uphill battle. Because they can ramp IKZ faster and have access to big late game entities like Sandcoil Python and Teb Fea, you want to try to pressure their leader health as much as possible and as quickly as possible. Save your Pulled Under for the opposing Stone Masked Ancient (taunt card), stack up Foamback Crab in the alley so you can start using them to protect your bigger entities from opposing big late game entity swings to give them one more turn to deal damage, and play Soryu No Rin if they don’t have Teb Fea in their garden yet (or as a last resort).

vs fire, it’s also an uphill battle. Rushfire allows for lots of burst damage earlier in the game given the nature of the gate. Games tend not to last longer than turn 5 or 6 because of how many entities can be played via the Rushgate summon, and because of how low-cost the meta-relevant cards in its kit are. Your overall goal is to prolong the game as much as possible and keep up on health, and to prevent yourself from getting to 7 health by turn 5 - because one Detonation Pact and Fire Orb will take you down from there, with no defending it. Always count up how much maximum possible damage the opponent can do every turn, and play accordingly.

vs lightning, you also want to prolong the game. It’s important to note that you can safely assume that the opponent will be swinging for 5+ damage every turn from turn 4 and onward. Do your best to fill your board and find all the Foamback Crabs that you can. This matchup is solely why Mizuto isn’t a bad entity to run, with only 4 search targets- cycling through your deck with a combination of Mizuto, Hayabusa Saburo, Benzai, and Fumiko will give you the best possible chances of surviving those heavy opposing swings.



Final Thoughts
Shao is an extremely versatile and fun deck to run when you get the hang of it. Unlike most of the other decks in the current meta, your chances of finding your win condition with this deck increase as the game plays on, with all of the cards you have in your deck to search and draw more cards. Knowing what cards you need to get into your hand, and how to do so, is what separates good Shao players from great ones. Your win condition for every game is a little bit different - this deck has all the tools, but you will need to be creative to find and draw into them. 
All that being said, as of me writing this in the current post-Rushfire ban meta, Shao is not currently the best deck in format. It’s very good, but Raizan/Surge Gate currently probably has the most consistency to win games. Then again, Shao has never been the best deck - and I’ve always been okay with that, stubbornly playing Shao. I play to enjoy the game and if I happen to win as well, it’s a bonus. === miss-the-rage | Miss the Rage | A post-Rushfire ban build for Fire
Lose the thing that makes everyone come over and what do you have left?
Consistency and tools → Control burn and absolute domination if you can survive.
Scorchweaver searchables → 15
Pyreskin searchables → 13
They will expect you to use the Rage Gate, so depending on how they respond, the Gate is already being respected. You don't have to use it every time. Just having it available can slow the game down significantly—unless they're Lightning.

Go wide, clear, and control the board from turns 1–3.

If they don't have a board, keep pressuring their life.
If they establish a board, shift your focus toward control.
If their one entity can't clear your board, it's perfectly fine to leave it alive and continue pressuring their life instead.

This build is focused on ending the game around turns 6–7, with the goal of establishing complete board domination between turns 3–5.
You should average around 7–10 damage per turn after turn 5, so your main objective is to control the board during the midgame while getting them down to around 10 life. From there, your late-game damage should be enough to close things out.

Bait those Sunderings early or play safe the whole game. Depending on your hand, you can go either way.

Fire Mirror
People are neglecting Fire right now, but it's still important to run some Sundering Strike for the mirror.
Your best bet is to save your extra token or hold your resources for turns 4–6 to keep them at bay.
Don't waste your answers early if you don't have to. You want enough resources left to shut them down when the game starts to swing too hard.
Lightning → The Weakness
The downside of this build is that it can be a little too slow into Lightning.
However, after testing, I've found that a few Sunderings, healing, and a Mocking are usually enough to slow them down to the point where the matchup starts favoring you.

Earth → Wide, Then Big Damage
This matchup doesn't have many issues.
You're naturally wide enough to poke them early, while your midgame control keeps their defenders from becoming a major problem. You also have enough Charge and late-game entities that can go over their defenders, over-damage their board, and pressure their life at the same time.
The plan is simple: go wide early, control the midgame, then overwhelm them with bigger damage.

Go for face and clear their units with spells and pings.

It's also a very situational tool. If there's a turn where you need to ping yourself twice for whatever reason, Lounge Siren becomes an incredibly useful piece to have available.
Most Slept-On → Omen Peddler
The most slept-on character in this build is probably Omen Peddler.
Clear + Threat next turn.
Omen Peddler keeps most enemies at bay while forcing your opponent to focus on him instead of your life total. That extra pressure relief is exactly what this deck wants while it's setting up its midgame.
Conclusion
To sum it up:
Early-game wide boards + plenty of control tools + strong game-ending spells, weapons, and big bodies.
You're not trying to win the game immediately.
You're trying to survive the early game, force them to spend their answers, take control around turns 3–5, and then completely overwhelm them by turns 6–7.
Another formidable opponent is Benzai Hydromancy, but you will have the tools for this match up also.
